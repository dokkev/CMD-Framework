## Question

CoDe's hypothesis is that a crossed four-bar finger takes different contact
modules (longer, thicker pads) with less change to the hand than the
alternatives: the 2R-DD, the belt 2R and the RCJ.

The two studies split the question:

| Study | Question |
|---|---|
| Study 1, contact interface capacity | Which modules does each frozen hand carry? |
| Study 2, morphology preservation | For a module a hand does not carry, how far must the hand change? |

## Formulation

### References

There is one external morphology, the current prototype's:

| Descriptor | Value |
|---|---|
| finger length L | 90.0 mm |
| proximal span \|OA″\| | 43.3 mm |
| distal span \|B″C\| | 30.2 mm |
| articulation | 55.05 mm |

The prototype is stored as a tracked design vector in
`design/scripts/study/study2_morphology_adaptation/references.yaml`, with its
provenance: run_004, candidate 2113. Every architecture is built to this
morphology once, in its own way.

| Reference | Actuation |
|---|---|
| CoDe (the prototype itself) | servo tilt 10° |
| 2R-DD | servo 0° and 90° |
| belt 2R | servo tilt 10°, 6 mm pulleys |
| RCJ | servo 0° and 90° |
| ideal RCJ | none at the joint; no roller wall, bearing or cable retention. **An upper bound, reported apart** |

All references share the whole goal, except their actuation (the servo tilt
and the pulleys). This includes:
- the required flexion;
- the finger length range;
- the mounting policy (`shallowest_supported`, no spacer);
- pins, servo envelopes and pads;
- the face rules;
- the resolution.

`shared.check_comparable` stops the study if any reference differs.

### Modules

There are 71 pairs: named pairs, plus grids over length 15–45 mm and
thickness 3/5/8 mm, varying the proximal side, the distal side or both.

### Physics

Each mechanism's own `evaluate()`. This is a 2D side-view geometric and
kinematic model that checks:
- collisions through the motion (pads, pins, links, rollers, servos);
- the flexion reached;
- each pad's usable length at the required flexion.

The usable length is the face up to the fold, less any part a crossing link
or the belt passes in front of (`links_block_face`).

### Study 1

The design is frozen. Only the module and its policy depth change.

- **Mounted**: the policy gives a depth, and the module fits its segment's
  support room. This is the same test for every mechanism.
- **Feasible**: it reaches the required flexion φ_req ∈ {90, 120, 150}° with
  no collision and both pads usable.

### Study 2

The hand is H = (E, Q, A), with the contact module C fixed per target:
- E: the four external descriptors above;
- Q: the mechanism's own coordinates, independent of E. These are a_x, a_z,
  b_z, bc_length and bp_x for CoDe, the roller radius for the RCJ, and none
  for the 2R-DD and the belt 2R;
- A: actuation, held fixed.

Costs are normalised by the reference L and never summed:
- ε_E = max|Δe|/L;
- ε_Q = max|Δq|/L.

The hierarchy runs in this order, at φ_req = 120°:

| Step | What changes |
|---|---|
| Frozen | nothing |
| Level 1 | min ε_Q with E fixed |
| Level 2a | min ε_E |
| Level 2b | min ε_Q subject to ε_E ≤ ε_E* + 0.1/90 |

Each target has an **E floor**: max(0, 9.5 + l_p − |OA″|, l_d − |B″C|)/L.
It is the span a module forces on any mechanism. The **excess** is ε_E minus
the floor, and the differences between architectures show in the excess.

Reported results are the best of 3 seeds under the same lexicographic rule.
The CSVs keep the spread across seeds.

## Results

### Study 1: what a frozen hand carries

Number of the 71 pairs carried (`study1_matched_comparison`,
`study1_coverage_vs_rom`):

| Reference | Mounted | 90° | 120° | 150° | φ_max (baseline modules) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CoDe | 38 | 37 | **35** | **28** | 176° |
| 2R-DD 0° | 32 | 27 | 17 | 0 | 130° |
| 2R-DD 90° | 32 | 32 | 31 | 13 | 166° |
| belt 2R | 38 | 37 | 33 | 17 | 166° |
| RCJ 0° | 38 | 30 | 20 | 5 | 165° |
| RCJ 90° | 38 | 32 | 32 | 22 | 180° |
| *ideal RCJ (upper bound)* | *38* | *37* | *34* | *26* | *180°* |

- **Why modules do not mount.** At this morphology the support room sets
  mounting.
  - The 33 pairs longer than the spans mount on no architecture. A proximal
    pad needs 9.5 mm + l_p ≤ 43.3 mm, and a distal pad needs l_d ≤ 30.2 mm.
  - The 2R-DD (both tilts) also loses 6 pairs within the spans, where the
    policy gives no depth (`not_mounted:no_depth`). The likely cause is the
    distal pad meeting the joint servo housing. This was diagnosed, not
    saved.
  - Every other architecture mounts every pair within the spans.
- **Why mounted modules fail at 120°.** Counts are by the largest violated
  constraint; many failures also violate a second one.
  - CoDe: 3 pairs (2 on pad usability, 1 on ROM).
  - Belt 2R: 5, all on pad usability.
  - RCJ 90°: 6, all on ROM.
  - RCJ 0°: 18 (11 on pad usability, 7 on ROM).
  - 2R-DD 0°: 15 (9 on ROM, 6 on pad usability).
- **CoDe's distal 30 × 3 mm module fails, but 30 × 5 and 30 × 8 pass**
  (`study1_accommodation_maps`). A thin pad's face lies near the link line,
  so the crossing link passes in front of it. A thicker pad lies further
  palmar and clears the link.
- **The gap above the others is small.** CoDe carries the most pairs at 120°
  and 150°, even above the ideal RCJ, but the lead is narrow at 120°: 2 over
  the belt 2R and 3 over the RCJ at 90°. The lead grows with φ_req: at 150°,
  CoDe carries 28 against 22 for the RCJ at 90° and 17 for the belt 2R.

### Study 2: the least change to carry each target

The table shows the best of 3 seeds (`study2_adaptation_costs`). Each cell
gives the excess of ε_E over the floor and, in brackets, ε_Q when it is
non-zero:
- *frozen* means the hand carries the target unchanged;
- "—" means no seed found a design.

| Target (floor) | CoDe | 2R-DD 0° | 2R-DD 90° | belt 2R | RCJ 0° | RCJ 90° | *ideal RCJ* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| thick (0) | frozen | 0.008 | frozen | frozen | 0.017 | frozen | *frozen* |
| long (0.053) | **0** [Q 0.080] | 0.130 | 0.034 | **0** | 0.096 | 0.093 | *0* |
| long_thick (0.053) | **0.054** [Q 0.059] | — | 0.140 | — | 0.151 | 0.151 | *0.038 [Q 0.018]* |
| asym_long_proximal (0.069) | — | 0.100 | **0** | — | 0.010 | 0.010 | *0* |
| asym_long_distal (0.109) | **0** [Q 0.001] | 0.059 | **0** | **0** | 0.012 | **0** | *0* |
| asym_proximal_heavy (0.124) | — | 0.001 | **0** | — | **0** | **0** | *0* |
| asym_distal_heavy (0.164) | **0** | 0.004 | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** | *0* |

Wherever a design was found, all three seeds found one. Level 1
(mechanism-only change, E fixed) never succeeded for CoDe or the RCJ on any
target. The 2R-DD and the belt 2R have no Q to change.

**Long modules.**
- CoDe reaches the floor on long and on the distal-dominant targets, and is
  the only real architecture within 0.06 of the floor on long_thick.
- On long it pays a mechanism change (ε_Q 0.080 ≈ 7 mm on its own
  coordinates) that the belt 2R and the ideal RCJ do not.
- The seeds disagree on CoDe's two hardest targets:
  - long: ε_E 0.053–0.077, ε_Q 0.078–0.094;
  - long_thick: ε_E 0.108–0.141, ε_Q 0.042–0.169.
- On long_thick only the best seed is within 0.06 of the floor. The worst
  seed is 0.088 above it, which is still ahead of the other real
  architectures (0.14 or more, or no design).
- With the face rule off, CoDe reaches the floor on long with ε_Q 0. Its
  0.080 mechanism change on long is therefore also caused by the face rule,
  not only its proximal failures.

**Long proximal modules (`links_block_face`).** CoDe finds no design for
asym_long_proximal or asym_proximal_heavy.

- **Cause.** The crossing links pass in front of the longer proximal face at
  120°, and that part of the face is not counted as usable (`pad_usable_mm`).
  With the face rule off (sensitivity run), CoDe reaches the floor on both,
  with ε_Q 0.022 and 0.021, and on long_thick with ε_Q 0.310.
- **The cost is architectural.** The rule is a shared comparison rule, and
  the belt 2R obeys it too. It was not relaxed in the main results.

**Belt 2R.**
- It fails the same proximal targets, and also long_thick, **with or without
  the face rule**.
- **These failures are a search-domain artifact, not a property of the
  architecture.** Study 2 holds the belt's servo shaft in place, so moving
  the joint A changes `ap_from_a_x_mm`. That coordinate keeps its sampling
  box of [−20, 30] mm, and the matched reference sits at −17.55 mm, so the
  joint can move only 2.45 mm distally. The long proximal targets need the
  joint to move more than that.
- **Correction (2026-11-07).** An earlier version of this report blamed the
  failures on the proximal pad hitting the distal pad at the fold. That came
  from a fixed-coordinate probe. The compact extension
  (`study2_compact_2026-11-07.md`) widens this box to the full joint domain
  and finds belt designs for all three targets.

**2R-DD and RCJ.**
- With the servo standing (90°), both carry most targets at or near the
  floor.
- On long and long_thick they need large span changes (excess 0.03–0.15),
  because the joint servo sits where the longer pads must go.
- Laying the servo back (0°) is worse for both.

**Representative adaptations.** `study2_before_after` shows:
- CoDe on long_thick;
- 2R-DD 90° on long;
- belt 2R on asym_distal_heavy;
- RCJ 90° on asym_long_proximal.

## Is CoDe's hypothesis supported?

**Partly.** Within the stated scope:

**Supported.**
- **Most module pairs carried.** At the shared morphology, CoDe carries the
  most module pairs (35/71 at 120°, 28/71 at 150°).
- **Long and thick together.** On long_thick it is the real architecture
  closest to the floor: excess 0.054 at the best seed and 0.088 at the
  worst. The 2R-DD and the RCJ need 0.14–0.15 extra, or fail.
- **Large flexion.** Its lead over the others grows as φ_req increases.

**Not supported.**
- **Long proximal modules.** CoDe fails asym_long_proximal and
  asym_proximal_heavy outright. The cause is a direct consequence of its
  architecture: the crossing links pass in front of the proximal face. The
  standing-servo 2R-DD carries both targets at the floor. The standing-servo
  RCJ carries them at excess 0.010 and 0.
- **Long and distal-dominant modules do not set CoDe apart.** CoDe reaches
  the floor on these targets, but so do the belt 2R, the standing-servo
  2R-DD and RCJ, and the ideal RCJ, mostly with ε_Q 0. On long, CoDe needs
  ε_Q 0.080 and the belt 2R needs none. This shows CoDe matches the others,
  not that it needs less change.
- **Small margins.** The margins over the belt 2R and the standing-servo RCJ
  are small (2–3 pairs at 120°). One shared morphology was compared, so these
  margins are not general.

In Study 2, the ideal RCJ matches or beats CoDe on every target. In Study 1
it carries fewer pairs (34 against 35 at 120°, 26 against 28 at 150°), but
reaches a higher φ_max (180° against 176°). It is an upper bound with no roller hardware modelled, and
is not evidence against CoDe.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Model.** A 2D side-view kinematic and geometric model with Y layers
  assumed. It does not cover tolerances, bearings, fasteners, strength,
  cable or belt tension, or 3D assembly. A feasible result shows that the
  planar room exists, not that the design can be built.
- **Fixed mounting and actuation.** Mounting is the shared policy with no
  spacer, and actuation is fixed per reference (Option 1 scope). A spacer or
  a moved servo would change the 2R-DD and belt "does not mount" results.
- **One morphology.** Results are for the prototype's morphology. Native
  references (each architecture's own optimum) are defined but were not run.
- **"Not found" is not "impossible".** It is the result of three NSGA-II
  seeds at population 100 and 100 generations. CoDe's seed spread on long and
  long_thick shows that its search is harder. A larger budget could lower its
  costs or find designs that were not found.
- **The face rule is a modelling choice.** The `links_block_face` rule counts
  any link or belt passing in front of a face as blocking, whatever the
  distance. The sensitivity run shows that it decides CoDe's proximal
  results.
- **The belt's sampling box.** Its "not found" results come from the
  relative box on `ap_from_a_x_mm` (see the belt results above). They are not
  evidence about the belt architecture.
- **The 2R-DD and belt 2R have no Q.** For them ε_Q is identically 0, so all
  their adaptation shows in ε_E.
- **Uncommitted code.** The runs were made on a dirty tree. The exact code is
  saved with each run (`source.patch`, `source_untracked/`).
