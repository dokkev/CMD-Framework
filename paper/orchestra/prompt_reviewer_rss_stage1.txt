## What Stage 1 is (from the RSS 2027 Call for Papers and Reviewer Guidelines)
- The submission is a 6-page Extended Abstract: at most 5 pages of content and 1 page of references. Nothing beyond
  that is reviewed; there is no appendix and no supplementary material.
- It is a semi-complete paper: "while the problem statement, hypotheses, and methodology must be fully fleshed out, it
  is expected that experiments or empirical validations will be missing or preliminary."
- "Reviewers and Area Chairs (ACs) will evaluate the paper's hypothesis and methodology." Your aim is "to judge the
  paper's hypothesis in terms of how relevant and impactful it is and how feasible it is to provide support for the
  hypothesis or not."
- "A hypothesis is judged by its scientific value, meaning that 'negative' results are valued equally with positive
  ones." Missing hardware or missing experiments are not, by themselves, reasons to reject at Stage 1; they belong in
  the Extension Charter if they are needed to support or refute the hypothesis.
- The related work should cite only literature directly tied to the paper's central problem and novelty; judge
  citations by direct relevance, not comprehensiveness.
- Papers that pass Stage 1 are invited to a rebuttal; the Area Chair then turns the reviews into an Extension Charter
  (required clarifications, evaluations and methodological extensions) that the authors execute for Stage 2.

## The review form
1. Value of Hypothesis (score 1-4)
   - 4 Strongly Interesting: an excellent hypothesis that will have significant impact on the community.
   - 3 Interesting.
   - 2 Possibly Interesting.
   - 1 Limited Interest: the hypothesis is of limited interest and is not likely to have impact on the community.
   Text ("Approach and Hypothesis"): state the hypotheses as you understand them, justify the score, and suggest
   concrete variations of the hypotheses that would make them more valuable.
2. Feasibility of Conclusion (score 1-4)
   - 4 Very Strong: the hypothesis can clearly be supported and provide very strong evidence for a conclusion.
   - 3 Good.
   - 2 Limited.
   - 1 Weak: it is very challenging to provide support for the hypothesis.
   Text ("Feasibility of Conclusion"): can the planned and presented evaluation support or falsify each hypothesis?
   Is the execution and interpretation plan clear? Which confounds or alternative explanations remain open?
3. Extension Charter: a numbered list of the clarifications, evaluations and methodological extensions the authors
   should carry out for Stage 2, each with its motivation. Keep it feasible and tied to the hypotheses.
4. Clarity notes: a numbered list of presentation and clarity items (text, equations, figures, tables) that would
   make the hypotheses, method and evidence easier to read. Be specific (section, figure, sentence).
5. Confidential Comments: anything for the AC only. Flag any break of anonymity, any reference that looks
   hallucinated (a citation that does not exist or does not say what it is cited for), plagiarism, and any format
   problem (page limit, template changes, appendix).
6. Recommendation: one of "Invite to Rebuttal" or "Reject".
