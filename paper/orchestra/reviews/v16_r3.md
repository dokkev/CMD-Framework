THOUGHT:

Reviewer background: planar mechanism kinematics, poles/instantaneous centres, four-bar synthesis, design optimization. I read all six pages and the figures, and re-derived every analytical statement.

What the paper claims. (C1) A finger with remote PIP actuation and a crossed four-bar (CFL) PIP keeps more pad coverage than a servo-at-joint revolute finger (2R-DD) at every pad thickness (3-9 mm) and required flexion (90/120/150 deg). (C2) When a finger is redesigned for thicker pads, what must be kept is the motion of the distal contact face relative to the proximal contact face (the PIP column of the contact Jacobian in the proximal-face frame), not the skeleton; a fixed axis cannot keep it (closed-form lower bound), a CFL gets to 10-51% of that bound, and in simulation the error in a measured interface effect (friction, stiffness, shape) under a reused command or torque follows Delta_contact (Spearman 0.75-0.94) and not Delta_skel or other candidates. Stage 2: one physical CoDe finger with swappable pads and CFL links (O, R, U).

Mathematics, checked line by line.
- Lemma 1: (I - R_q)^{-1} = (1/(4 sin^2(q/2))) [(1 - cos q) I + sin q R_{pi/2}] = (1/2)[I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}]. So P = (1/2)(b + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2} b). Correct.
- Prop. 1: composing a reflection in the vertical line through P with a rotation by q about P gives a reflection in the line through P turned by q/2; the deep joint-side face corner is the extreme point toward the line for q in (0, pi), so the condition is G/2 >= (t - z) tan(q/2), which is Eq. (2) with [.]_+. Correct, and it needs only equal face depth plus symmetric joint-side corners, not equal thickness or equal length (the authors could say this). It needs q_req < pi, which holds. Fig. 2 numbers check: 5 tan60 = 8.66, 9 tan60 = 15.6, 4 tan60 = 6.93. 2 tan(q/2) = 2.0 / 3.46 / 7.46 mm at 90/120/150 deg. Correct.
- Prop. 2: the perpendicular distance from the proximal phalanx end (x_P - G/2, 0) to the mirror line is (G/2) cos(q/2) + z sin(q/2), so the ends are G cos(q/2) + 2 z sin(q/2) apart and L_f(q) = L_f - G(1 - cos(q/2)) + 2 z sin(q/2). Correct.
- The "lower the pole by delta" argument: numerator gains 2 delta tan(Q) with Q = q_req/2; the denominator at q_req gains 2 delta tan Q (1 - cos Q) + 2 delta sin Q = 2 delta tan Q exactly. A neat identity, and (N + a)/(D + a) > N/D iff N < D. At q < q_req the denominator gains less (the difference is sin(theta - Q)/cos Q < 0), so C-bar rises even more. Correct, but only inside the idealization (S1, S2, equal pads, G tight at q_req, max of G_sym reached at q_req). "The best joint turns about a point on the contact face" is a property of this model, not of a real finger with pad compliance and link envelopes, and should be worded that way.
- Proposition 3: Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and ||I - R_q|| = 2 sin(q/2) times a rotation. Correct.
- Eq. (7): the pole relative to the face and relative to the link line differ by the face depth, so Delta p_contact - Delta p_skel = (0, -Delta t). The triangle inequality at q_req gives the bound. Correct, given that the proximal face is parallel to the link line and the two frames share the x origin (both tacit). Consistency check on Fig. 1b,c (3 -> 7 mm, bound 2 sin60 * 4 = 6.93 mm): U 3.4 + 3.9 = 7.3, R 7.7 + 0.9 = 8.6. Both satisfy (7), and U is close to tight.
- Corollary 1: correct. The maximum over q <= q_req sits at q_req because q_req <= pi. RCJ: I checked z = r tan(q/4) numerically (r = 1, q = 90 deg gives P = (0, 0.414)). Correct. Contrast family: Delta_contact = 2 sin60 Delta h = sqrt(3) Delta h, consistent. Fig. 4 numbers are consistent with the bound (e.g. 0.35/3.46 = 10% at 120 deg / 5 mm, 4.31/8.49 = 51% at 90 deg / 9 mm).
- Holm: five p <= 0.0006 < 0.05/25 = 0.002, so they survive any step-down order. Correct.
- Imprecision: "The finite pole equals the instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis" holds as an identity in q, but the two coincide in the limit q -> 0 and can coincide at isolated q. The text also uses "the pole" for both the finite pole (Lemma 1, Props. 1-3) and the instantaneous centre (Sec. III-C, column [R_{pi/2}(x - p_hat); 1]). Both are legitimate, since keeping one path is equivalent to keeping the other given c0. It should still be stated once, because the reader has to work it out.

So the analytical core is correct and compact. Kinematically, though, it is classical: keeping c(q) and phi = q for all q means keeping the relative motion of the two contact faces, i.e. keeping the fixed and moving centrodes relative to the faces. The exact fix is a translation of all pivots by Delta h in both bodies, which is the conjugation T_v D T_{-v} with pole P + v. The paper's own "pivots would have to move into the pad" says exactly this. Calling it "keeping the contact Jacobian" is a relabelling that hides the standard synthesis tools (centrode or Burmester-type approximate synthesis of a translated pole path). The contribution is the framing (which invariant a redesign for an interface study should preserve) plus the evidence. The kinematic results are not new as kinematics.

The most substantive technical concern is the choice of metric. Delta_contact is the displacement of one material point c. For an object held between two flat faces (the Study 3 setting, a cylinder in the V), the grasp geometry depends only on the two face lines, and sliding the distal face along itself changes nothing in the V. From Prop. 3, the normal offset of the distal face line is n_q . Delta c = ((R_q - I) n) . Delta p. This is 2 sin(q/2) times the projection of Delta p onto one direction. The orthogonal component of Delta p only slides the distal face along itself. For a pure depth shift of the pole, the face-line offset is (1 - cos q) Delta z, against Delta_contact = 2 sin(q/2) Delta z (1.0 vs 1.41 at 90 deg, 1.5 vs 1.73 at 120 deg). The paper itself has to remove a "start offset" for 2R-DD and RCJ "because their contact point moved along the face" (Fig. 4d). That is exactly the tangential component the metric should not count. Delta_contact is therefore a plausible proxy, but the paper has not shown it is the right quantity: the face-line offset, or the inscribed-object centre displacement, is the obvious sixth candidate and is missing from Table I. If it predicts as well or better, C2 should be stated in terms of face-line motion.

Study 3 design. (a) In the main settings all pads share one contact model, so thickness acts only through geometry. Geometry then predicting the error is close to built in. The softening-pad counterfactual is a good addition and partly answers this. (b) The hand set is constructed: R, U, and "redesigns at set Delta_contact that keep or change the skeleton as much as possible". By Eq. (7), designs near the bound have Delta_skel close to const - Delta_contact, so the negative Delta_skel correlations (-0.40 to -0.51) come partly from the construction. The hand-independent grid (45 hands, mutual |rho| <= 0.40, partial 0.86) is the right answer and should be the headline. (c) Samples are small (14-16 hands; shape over 6 hands). (d) The fixed-axis family, the cleanest test of "keep the skeleton exactly" (Delta_skel = 0, large Delta_contact), is excluded from the correlations ("outside the correlations above"). Why? It lives on a different reference O, but this should be justified. (e) q0 + 5 deg reaches 125 deg, beyond q_req = 120 deg, where Delta_contact is defined. Minor.

The effect sizes also temper C2. R still errs 41% at 9 mm under a reused command and 50% at 9 mm with soft pads. Under reused torque the R/U gap is only about 2x (5/17/12 vs 9/28/23%). So "keep the contact Jacobian" is achieved partially, and how much it matters depends on the protocol. Matching contact force removes most of the error (3-7%). The spacer alternative (Delta_contact = 0 exactly, at 8.6-18.2 points of coverage for thinner pads) is the trivial baseline whenever the interface set is known. The paper says it studies only the redesign a new interface forces, which is fair, but that narrows the scope of C2 and should be stated as a scope condition in C2 itself.

C1 design-optimization concerns. Coverage is the only objective and mechanical advantage is not constrained. The CFL optima have worst-case MA 0.41-0.58 with "the same actuators", so the CFL finger roughly halves the available PIP torque against 2R-DD. That is a real cost of the claimed room, and C1 should be stated with it, or the comparison repeated at matched MA or fingertip force. The parameterizations differ in richness (four pivot coordinates versus one axis), and the CFL got an extra refinement (the "also without the refinement" statement helps). There are only three seeds and no seed spread, so a 2.4-point margin needs its spread reported for "exceeds in all 10 conditions" to mean anything. The ablation (2R-remote equals or beats CFL at 90 deg, CFL wins from 120 deg) is honest and informative.

Stage 1 completeness. The hypotheses are falsifiable and stated before the evidence, thresholds were fixed before the runs, and post hoc analyses are labelled. Good practice. The Stage 2 plan is thinner than the simulation. The C1 physical test ("fails if an exchange needs the articulation taken apart") is a design-feature check, not a test of the coverage claim. The C2 test compares only R vs U at 7 mm with a one-sided Wilcoxon. It does not test the Delta_contact-error relation the paper argues for. There is no power estimate. The marker tolerance on Delta_contact is not specified, yet R's change is 0.9 mm. How the limit load is measured on the planar slide, and whether the textured cover changes pad thickness or stiffness, is not specified.

Related work omits the most directly relevant kinematics and finger-design literature: underactuated and linkage fingers with phalanx (enveloping) cylinder grasps and contact-force distribution over the proximal and distal phalanges (Birglen, Laliberte, Gosselin), and ICR/centrode-based finger and prosthetic joint synthesis beyond the one knee paper. The Montana/Murray citation for "contact Jacobian" is fine, but the paper's J is only the PIP column in the proximal-face frame, which is narrower than the usual meaning.

Presentation. Dense and telegraphic. Many symbols appear briefly or without definition (x0, r_{1,e}, r_{2,s}, "transmission table", "start offset", "contrast family", "impedance ratio"). L_f is overloaded as a constant and as L_f(q). S2 holds "only roughly" for real CFL designs (up to 1.6 mm). The figures are small but readable. The abstract is packed with numbers.

Verdict. The math is correct, the framing is useful, the hypotheses are falsifiable and the simulation evidence is careful, with robustness and preregistration. The weaknesses are the metric choice (material point vs face line), the near-tautology and construction confounds in Study 3, the unaccounted MA cost in C1, and a Stage 2 plan that tests less than the paper claims. For a Stage 1 extended abstract this clears the bar, with the requested revisions. Overall 6, Accept, confidence 4.
