THOUGHT:
I read this as a mechanism-kinematics reviewer and re-derived the design section by hand before judging the studies.

Mathematics, checked by hand.
- Lemma 1. (I - R_q)^{-1} = (1/2)(I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}) follows from s/(1-c) = cot(q/2). Correct for q in (0, 2pi).
- Proposition 1. A rotation by q about P of the q=0 mirror image is a reflection in the line through P turned by q/2. A convex set and its mirror image are disjoint iff the set stays strictly on one side. For the deepest joint-side corner at (-a, -(d+h-z)) relative to the pole this gives a >= (d+h-z) tan(q/2), so G = 2a. Eq. (2) is right. Fig. 2 numbers check: 5 tan60 = 8.66, 9 tan60 = 15.59, 4 tan60 = 6.93; 2 tan60 = 3.46; tan75/tan45 = 3.73 ("nearly four times").
- Proposition 2. Numerator: mirror pads give T1 = T2, 2|T1| = l1 + l2 = L_f - x0 - G. Phalanx ends at (-/+G/2, z) from the pole are 2|n.p| = G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2) apart. Eq. (3) is right. The remark that lowering the pole by delta adds 2 delta tan(q_req/2) to numerator and denominator alike also checks: 2 delta tan(Q/2)(1 - cos(Q/2)) + 2 delta sin(Q/2) = 2 delta tan(Q/2). At q < q_req the denominator grows less, so the mean coverage also rises. All of this assumes z constant in q.
- Proposition 3. Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, norm 2 sin(q/2)|Delta p|. Correct. Eq. (7) is a triangle inequality on two pole shifts that differ by Delta t in depth, evaluated at q_req. Correct, given that both faces deepen by the same Delta t and both start points are kept. Fig. 1 is consistent with it: bound 2 sin60 * 4 = 6.93 mm; U gives 3.4 + 3.9 = 7.3, R gives 7.7 + 0.9 = 8.6.
- Corollary 1. I derived the finite pole of two equal rollers: it sits at (r, r tan(q/4)) from the proximal roller centre, so z = r tan(q/4) holds and does not depend on face depth. The corollary extends to the RCJ as stated. Fig. 4 bound at 150 deg, Delta h = 6 mm: 2 sin75 * 6 = 11.6 mm, matches the label.

So the mathematics is correct. It is also elementary (planar finite-displacement theory, Bottema and Roth), and the paper cites it as such. The value is in the application: one quantity, the finite pole, gives both the clearance cost and the contact-path change.

Where propositions and studies do not line up.
1. S1 and S2 are strong. The CoDe finger carries a PIP servo on the proximal phalanx, and pads sit "at the shallowest depth clear of joint hardware", so d1 = d2 is unlikely in the very design the paper proposes. A four-bar's finite pole also moves along x, so S2 cannot hold at every q for fixed pads. The Fig. 3 caption reports Eq. (2) within 0.05 mm median and 1.6 mm worst, and designs are judged by exact clearance, which is the right safeguard. But the propositions then explain the trend; they do not prove the Study 1 result.
2. "Its finite pole can lie palmar of every physical pivot" is asserted, not shown. In my roller derivation the finite pole (depth r tan(q/4)) is shallower than the instantaneous centre (depth r sin(q/2)). For a crossed four-bar the instantaneous centre is the crossing point of the two links, inside the pivot hull. I could not verify the claim from the text; a plot of z(q) for the CoDe linkage would settle it.
3. The title and C2 say "contact Jacobian", but Delta_contact is a max-norm path difference. They agree only at zero. A small Delta_contact does not bound the difference in J.
4. Delta_contact in Study 2 mixes a start-point offset with the pole change (Fig. 4d: 2R-DD and RCJ start apart). Proposition 3 assumes c0 kept. The caption gives the corrected count (7 of 9 cells), which is honest, but the headline 10-51% is for the mixed quantity.
5. H2 is weak as a test: the bound applies to fixed axes, so a polycentric joint below it is expected. The informative result is the coverage cost (within 0.2 points), not the pass/fail criterion. The 10-51% range is wide.
6. Study 3's own post hoc result says the error follows the signed normal offset of the distal face (rho = -0.99), and that the predicted rise with Delta_contact itself did not appear. Under a position-plus-stiffness setpoint this is close to what the contact model must give: preload is K times the interference. Delta_contact, an unsigned max norm over all q, is then a sufficient proxy, not the causal variable. A weaker, cheaper condition (keep the normal face offset at the grasp pose) may do the same job and may be reachable by a fixed axis. The paper should say so.
7. H1 compares against 2R-DD only. 2R-remote beats the CFL at 90 deg (Fig. 3), and the paper says so. C1 is then two separate effects, and the four-bar's part is shown only at 120 and 150 deg. Mechanical advantage is unconstrained and the CFL lands at 0.41-0.58; a fair comparison would hold it as a constraint. Only CFL gets a refinement pass, though the caption says it leads without it.
8. Q_eval collapses to one pose at q_req = 90 deg.
9. Statistics: 14-15 hands, candidates collinear by construction (thickness, coverage, Delta_contact all grow with h), designs derived from common parents. The grid of 27 helps. Under torque and touch protocols the skeleton predicts as well or better (0.71, 0.88), so the principle is specific to reused impedance setpoints. The paper states this.
10. Small inconsistencies: true change -37% in the text against -41% in the abstract and Fig. 1d; 3.9x and 0.7x in Fig. 1 against 3.3x and 0.75x in the text; "M" in the Stage 2 protocol is never defined as a hand.

Stage 1 fit. Problem, hypotheses with failure conditions, and a Stage 2 protocol with failure conditions are present. Negative outcomes are discussed (the "as we had predicted" admission, alternative protocols). No hardware data yet, which Stage 1 allows. The evidence is simulation with one contact model, one object class, one planar grasp, MCP held.

Presentation. Very dense. Sec. III is readable and the figures help. Study 3 packs too many settings, letters and numbers into one page; Table I's caption carries a methods section.

Decision. The mathematics is right, the framing is fresh, and the hypotheses are falsifiable. The weaknesses are a proxy metric that the paper's own analysis half-undermines, strong symmetry assumptions, and simulation-only evidence. For a Stage 1 abstract I lean accept, at 6.
