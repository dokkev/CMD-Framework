THOUGHT:
I read this as a mechanism-kinematics reviewer, so I spent most of the effort on Section III and on whether the design studies actually test what the propositions say.

Mathematics, checked by hand.
- Lemma 1: (I - R_q)^{-1} = (1/2)(I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}); correct. "Finite pole equals the instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis": from b = (I - R)P one gets p_hat = P + R_{pi/2}(I - R_q) P'(q), so the two coincide exactly where P' = 0. Correct as a statement over an interval of q.
- Proposition 1: reflection in the vertical line through the pole followed by rotation by q about the pole is a reflection in the line through the pole turned by q/2, so the mirror argument is right, and it shows what S2 really is: the pole's x must stay at the gap midpoint for all q. The signed distance of the face corner (x_m - G/2, depth t) from the mirror line gives G >= 2(t - z) tan(q/2). Eq. (2) is correct. Fig. 2 numbers (5 tan 60 = 8.66, 9 tan 60 = 15.6, 4 tan 60 = 6.93) and the 2.0 / 3.46 / 7.46 mm-per-mm slopes check out. S2 holds exactly for an antiparallelogram (the displacement is a product of two reflections whose axes meet on x = x_m) and only approximately for a general crossed four-bar; the paper says so (0.05 mm median, 1.6 mm worst).
- Proposition 2: the phalanx ends are G cos(q/2) + 2 z sin(q/2) apart; correct. The remark that lowering the pole by delta adds 2 delta tan(q_req/2) to both numerator and denominator at q_req is correct (tan(1 - cos) + sin = tan), and at the other evaluation poses the denominator grows by less, so the monotonicity claim holds for all of Q_eval.
- Proposition 3: c = (I - R_q)p + R_q c0, so Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and the norm is 2 sin(q/2)|Delta p|. Correct, and independent of which face point is chosen. Eq. (7) follows from Delta p_link - Delta p_face = Delta t n and the triangle inequality. Fig. 1 numbers satisfy it (3.4 + 3.9 and 7.7 + 0.9 against 6.93 mm).
- Corollary 1: correct for a fixed axis. For equal rollers I get the pole at (r, r tan(q/4)) below the link line, so the paper's z = r tan(q/4) is right. The contrast family's sqrt(3) Delta h is 2 sin 60. Correct.
So I found no mathematical error. The analysis is elementary but clean, and the propositions are stated with their assumptions.

Where I am less convinced.
1. The named quantity and the measured quantity differ. The paper's principle and title are about the contact Jacobian (a derivative, set by the instantaneous centre), while Delta_contact is a sup-norm distance between positions c(q), set by the finite pole from q = 0, and it includes a start offset c0 that is not a Jacobian difference at all (Fig. 4d). With equal c0 the two are equivalent as "exactly kept", but a small Delta_contact does not bound the change in c'(q). The Jacobian is also only the PIP column, in the proximal frame, with respect to flexion angle rather than actuator angle. The paper states each of these restrictions, but together they make "keeps the contact Jacobian" a much narrower statement than the title.
2. Transmission is defined out of J. For a crossed four-bar driven at a pivot the flexion-to-servo ratio is configuration dependent (worst-case MA 0.41-0.58). The simulation uses a flexion-space stiffness K0 and a lookup table, so transmission cannot matter there (rho about 0 in Table I is by construction). On a real position-controlled servo the reflected stiffness and torque scale with the ratio squared / the ratio, and this changes with each relinking. The Stage 2 protocol does not say how this is separated from Delta_contact.
3. H2 is weak as a falsifiable statement. A bound derived for a fixed axis is undercut by any joint with a free pole depth once Delta_contact is an optimization objective; the informative part is "at near-best coverage" and the comparison with the other joints' fronts. Also the exact solution is trivial kinematically (translate the whole joint by Delta h with the face), and the paper says so; the real content is the packaging constraint, and that constraint (servo envelope, link and pivot sizes, pad width, parameter bounds) is not given anywhere. For a Stage 1 paper where the method must be complete, the design space is under-specified.
4. Study 1's H1 is tested against the weakest baseline. Fig. 3 shows 2R-remote above CFL at 90 degrees for every thickness and at 120 degrees for thin pads. The paper reports this honestly as an ablation, but C1 as worded ("remote servos and a crossed four-bar keep more contact surface") is really two effects, and the four-bar only wins for deep flexion and thick pads. RCJ's very low real coverage against a high ideal bound is not explained.
5. Study 3/4 is close to a geometric tautology under a reused position command: hands whose faces follow the same relative path close on the object the same way. The more demanding torque protocol shrinks the error to 3-40% with n = 16 and a wide interval. Sample sizes are 14-16 hands in four of five columns, hands were selected to span Delta_contact and Delta_skel, and at fixed thickness the two are coupled through Eq. (7), so the candidates are not independent. Study 4 is post hoc (disclosed). The "true" effect is defined on O's own geometry, which builds the answer in to some degree.
6. Scope: MCP held at 0, planar, one object class, grasps between the proximal and distal pad of the same finger. The MCP column changes with pad depth and is acknowledged as not kept.

Presentation: the paper fits the 5 + 1 page limit, hypotheses each have a failure condition, and negative-outcome readings exist, which is what Stage 1 asks for. It is extremely compressed; Figs. 2, 3 and 5 are too small to read, several terms are used before or without definition (x0, S1/S2 as labels, mechanical advantage, "refines CFL from neighbouring designs", how O carries a thicker pad "without a redesign").

Overall: correct and tidy kinematics, a useful closed-form bound, an honest and pre-specified evaluation plan; modest novelty on the mechanism side, a narrow tested regime, and simulation-only evidence in which the main confound of a linkage joint (transmission) is idealised away. For Stage 1 I come out at a 6: accept, with the items above to be addressed for Stage 2.
