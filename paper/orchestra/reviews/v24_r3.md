ASSESSMENT NOTES:

Perspective: mechanism kinematics. I re-derived the design-section mathematics and checked whether Studies 1-3 test what Propositions 1-3 and Corollary 1 say.

Central claims and the evidence for each:

(C1) A remote-actuated crossed four-bar (CFL) PIP keeps more pad coverage than a servo-at-joint (2R-DD) finger at every pad thickness and required flexion. The geometry is sound.
- Lemma 1 is the standard finite-pole formula. I checked (I - R_q)^{-1} = 1/2 (I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}).
- Prop. 1: with flexion clockwise, two reflections give the mirror-line normal (cos q/2, -sin q/2). The binding point is the joint-side corner of the face, not the mount corner. Its signed distance gives exactly G > 2(d+h-z) tan(q/2), so Eq. (2) is correct under S1/S2.
- Prop. 2 is right. The phalanx ends end up G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2) apart (reflection of the end point about the same line), which gives Eq. (3).
- The "lower the pole by delta" argument is right. The denominator grows by 2 delta[tan(q/2)(1 - cos(q/2)) + sin(q/2)] = 2 delta tan(q/2), the same amount the numerator grows, and C < 1.
- The numbers in Fig. 2 and the text check out: 5 tan 60 = 8.7; 2 tan 60 = 3.46; tan 75 / tan 45 = 3.7.
- Study 1 tests H1 directly, with a falsification rule, a seed-robustness check (the worst of six CFL runs beats the best 2R-DD in 9 of 10 conditions) and an ablation (2R-remote). The ablation is informative. Remote actuation alone gives the room at 90 deg and in fact beats CFL there. The polycentric joint only pays off from about 120 deg up.
- So C1 as stated ("remote servos and a crossed four-bar") holds, but the four-bar's share holds only at deep flexion. The text says so; the contribution list should say it too.
- The price is mechanical advantage (0.41-0.58 vs 1). It is acknowledged but left out of the comparison.

(C2) A redesign should keep the contact-surface motion (the "contact Jacobian"), not the skeleton.
- Eq. (6) is correct: Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and ||I - R_q|| = 2 sin(q/2).
- Eq. (7) is correct: the face-frame and link-frame poles differ by Delta t along the depth direction, so |Delta p_f| + |Delta p_l| >= |Delta t|; evaluate at q_req.
- Corollary 1 follows directly. It is checked against Fig. 1 (3.9 + 3.4 and 0.9 + 7.7, both >= 6.93) and Fig. 4d (2R-remote is about 10.4 at 120 deg, 3 -> 9 mm, which is the bound).
- One error: the rolling-joint remark in the proof gives z = r tan(q/2). For two equal rollers, the finite pole of a flexion q stays at the initial contact point along the link line, at depth r tan(q/4). I confirmed this numerically: at 120 deg it is 0.577r, not 1.73r. The conclusion (z is fixed by r, so the bound holds) is unaffected, but the formula should be fixed.
- Study 2 tests H2 properly. It reports the coverage end of each front, which is conservative.
- The claim "least in every cell" relies on counting the start offset of 2R-DD and RCJ as part of Delta_contact. The Fig. 4 caption is honest that without the offset CFL is least in 7 of 9 cells. The body text should carry that qualifier.

Gap between the propositions and the hypothesis tested:
- The title and C2 speak of the contact Jacobian. Delta_contact is a C^0 path distance between contact points, not a Jacobian distance.
- Exact path equality does imply equal columns: with phi = q on every design, the instantaneous centre follows from c and c'. But a small Delta_contact does not bound the column error.
- Study 3's mechanism is plausibly zeroth-order: where the face sits at the setpoint, and hence the over-closure under a reused impedance target. The signed normal offset predicts the direction of the error with rho = -0.99.
- The partial correlations (0.64 with the setpoint force held fixed, 0.69 with the normal offset held fixed) help, but they do not show a first-order (velocity or Jacobian) effect.
- The finding is valuable either way. I would either rename the quantity (contact-surface path), or add a column-level metric, e.g. max ||c'(M) - c'(M0)||, and show it predicts the error too.

Study 3 statistics:
- They are careful: pre-registration, within-thickness bootstrap, Holm correction, partial correlations holding Delta_skel fixed.
- But n is small (14-15 hands per reused-setpoint column), and the CIs are wide ([0.41, 0.99], [0.35, 0.98]).
- The contact model is a point-contact normal stiffness, so pad thickness has no mechanical effect besides geometry.
- The "true" interface effect is defined on O with only the stiffness swapped. In a real compliant pad, thickness and stiffness are coupled.
- Table I is honest that under torque or force-matched protocols the skeleton matters (rho 0.71, 0.88). So C2 is protocol-specific, and the paper does scope it to "the setpoints servo hands run".

Stage-1 fit: the problem, hypotheses H1-H3, falsification rules and a Stage-2 hardware protocol (Page trend test over O, R, M, U) are all given, as the guidelines require.

Main points the scores rest on:
- The mathematics is correct apart from one minor formula.
- The studies line up with the propositions.
- Contribution: a useful, original framing (the pole decides both pad room and contact-surface invariance), with a simple but practical closed-form bound.
- Weaknesses:
  - terminology overreach ("Jacobian");
  - a protocol-specific C2;
  - simulation-only evidence with a simple contact model;
  - very dense presentation (Table I caption, Sec. V-B).
