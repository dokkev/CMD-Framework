THOUGHT:
I read this as a mechanism-kinematics reviewer and re-derived the design section by hand before looking at the studies.

Lemma 1. (I - R_q) = [[1-c, s], [-s, 1-c]], determinant 2(1-c), so the inverse is (1/2)(I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}) because s/(1-c) = cot(q/2). Correct.

Proposition 1 / Eq. (2). A corner at depth t = d + h below a pole at depth z must sit (t - z) tan(q/2) from the pole in x for the pad to stay on its side of the mirror line; two mirror-image pads give 2[t - z]_+ tan(q/2). The Fig. 2 numbers check: 5 tan 60 = 8.66, 9 tan 60 = 15.59, 4 tan 60 = 6.93, and the pad lengths 32/25/34 mm differ by about those amounts. 2 tan 60 = 3.46 mm per mm. tan 75 / tan 45 = 3.73, "nearly four times". All correct. The weak point is S2: with a moving pole, fixed pad corners cannot stay equidistant from the pole in x at every q, so the max over q in Eq. (2) is exact only for a pole that moves along the depth direction. The authors say S1, S2 hold "only roughly" and report the residual (0.05 mm median, 1.6 mm max), which is the right way to handle it, but the "iff" is stronger than what is proved.

Proposition 2 / Eq. (3). Phalanx ends at (-G/2, z) from the pole and its mirror are G cos(q/2) + 2 z sin(q/2) apart, so L_f(q) = L_f - G(1 - cos(q/2)) + 2 z sin(q/2); numerator l_1 + l_2 = L_f - x_0 - G. Correct, given G = L_vp (pads meet the phalanx ends). The statement after it I checked explicitly: lowering the pole by delta changes the denominator by 2 delta sin(q/2) + 2 delta tan(q/2)(1 - cos(q/2)) = 2 delta tan(q/2), the same as the gap reduction. Correct, and with C < 1 the ratio rises. This holds at q_req; at the other two evaluation poses the denominator grows by less, so the conclusion is the same sign. Good.

Proposition 3 / Eq. (6). Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, norm 2 sin(q/2) |Delta p|. Correct, and independent of the point chosen, which justifies "c stands for any such point". It needs the same c_0 on both fingers.

Eq. (7). The pole shift seen from the face and from the link line differ by Delta t along the normal, so their norms sum to at least |Delta t|; evaluating at q_req and noting that the Deltas are maxima over q gives the bound. Correct only if r_C is a skeleton-fixed point whose start is kept in the link-line frame. The text defines r_C as "the fingertip path written from the proximal link line instead of the face", which reads as the same material point in another frame. In that reading the start point shifts by Delta t and the argument only gives a sum >= |Delta t|, not 2 sin(q_req/2)|Delta t|. The Fig. 1 numbers (3.4 + 3.9 = 7.3 and 7.7 + 0.9 = 8.6, against 2 sin 60 x 4 = 6.93) are consistent with the stronger bound, so I believe the intended definition is the skeleton-fixed one, but the paper must say so.

Corollary 1 / Eq. (8). Normal component of the pole shift from the face is exactly Delta h - Delta d for a fixed axis; correct, with equality when there is no shift along the face. I verified the rolling-joint claim: for two equal rollers of radius r the finite pole is at (r, r tan(q/4)), so z = r tan(q/4). Correct. Bounds at 3 -> 9 mm are 8.49, 10.39, 11.59 mm at 90, 120, 150 degrees; Fig. 4 shows 2R-remote on the first two. CFL: 4.31 / 8.49 = 51% and 0.35 / 3.46 = 10%, matching the text.

So the mathematics is correct. I find no errors, two definitional gaps (S2 with a moving pole; r_C), and notation drift (P(q) in Lemma 1, p(q) in Prop. 3, p-hat for the instantaneous centre).

Do the studies test the propositions?
- Study 1 tests the consequence of Prop. 1-2 (coverage ordering) and reports how well Eq. (2) predicts the optimized CFL gap. It does not show directly that the optimized poles sit near the face (z close to d + h at q_req), which is the mechanism claimed. One number per condition would close this. Mechanical advantage is unconstrained, and CFL optima land at 0.41-0.58 against 1 for a pin joint, so the comparison is at equal actuators but not equal fingertip force. Only CFL gets a refinement stage, though the caption says the lead holds without it. At 90 degrees 2R-remote beats CFL, so the polycentric joint earns its place only at deep flexion; the text says so honestly, but C1 as worded credits both ingredients everywhere.
- Study 2 tests Corollary 1 well for 2R-remote (meets the bound exactly). For 2R-DD and RCJ the precondition "keep c_0" is violated (Fig. 4d: they start apart), so they fall below the dotted line for a reason outside the corollary; the caption explains 2R-DD by Delta d but not RCJ, whose curve at 90 degrees is below a bound that the corollary says holds for a rolling joint of fixed radius. Either the roller radius was re-optimized or c_0 moved; this needs one sentence. H2 itself is weakly falsifiable because a lower bound for fixed axes says nothing about a moving pole; the content is the coverage cost (0.2 points), which is reported.
- Study 3 tests whether Delta_contact predicts the error of a transferred measurement. Kinematically, Eq. (7) makes Delta_contact and Delta_skel trade off at fixed mount depth, so within the redesign set the two candidates are structurally anti-correlated; the negative rho of Delta_skel is partly a mirror of Delta_contact. The partial correlation with Delta_skel held fixed is mentioned, which helps. Delta_contact is a max over q <= 120 degrees while grasps close at 90-120 degrees, and Fig. 4d shows non-monotone ||Delta c(q)||, so a pose-specific Delta c would be the more direct candidate. The post hoc signed normal offset result (rho = -0.99 for direction) suggests the underlying mechanism is over-closure at a position setpoint, a near-static effect; the paper is candid about this and about the failed prediction on the reversed share. Under the "touch" protocol Delta_contact is not the predictor (0.44) and Delta_skel is (0.88); the claim is scoped to setpoint reuse and that scoping is carried through the abstract.

Other points. The title and C2 say "keep the contact Jacobian" but only the PIP column on the distal face is kept, the MCP column is explicitly not, and MCP is held in every study. The tolerance statement (about 1 mm for under 20%) sits next to R at 0.9 mm erring 21-25%. Everything is simulation with one contact model and one task; this is allowed at Stage 1, and the Stage 2 protocol with a Page trend test and explicit failure conditions for C1 and C2 satisfies the guideline on insights under negative results. Format: 5 pages of content, 1 of references, anonymous, related work narrow. Compliant.

Overall: a correct and compact kinematic argument (finite pole as the single quantity for both clearance and contact-surface motion), with studies that mostly test what is stated, and honest scoping. Weaknesses are definitional gaps, an equal-force caveat in Study 1, and simulation-only evidence. I rate it 6, Accept.
