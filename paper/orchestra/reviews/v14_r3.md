THOUGHT:
I read this as a mechanism-kinematics paper wrapped in a hand-design and measurement-validity story. It has two parts. C1 asks how much pad length the PIP joint takes, which is a clearance and coverage question governed by the finite pole. C2 asks how the relative motion of the two contact faces changes when the faces move deeper, and whether that change, rather than the change in skeleton motion, explains measurement error in interface studies. The pole is the right unifying object. Most of my effort went into checking the mathematics, because that is where I can add the most.

Checks of the mathematics (all done by hand):
- Lemma 1. In complex form, (1 - e^{iq})^{-1} = 1/2 + (i/2) cot(q/2), so P = 1/2 (b + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2} b). Correct.
- Prop. 1 (clearance). The distal pad at pose q is the proximal pad reflected in the vertical through P and then rotated by q about P. That composition is a reflection in the line through P turned by q/2. A closed convex set and its reflection intersect iff the set meets the mirror line, and a point on the line is its own image. So the "iff" is correct. For a fixed axis it gives a = t tan(q/2). The Fig. 2 numbers are consistent: 5 tan 60 = 8.66, 9 tan 60 = 15.59, 4 tan 60 = 6.93, and 2 tan 60 = 3.46 mm per mm. However, S2 (corners equidistant from the pole in x) must hold for every q in (0, q_req]. For a four-bar the pole's x-coordinate moves with q, so S2 holds exactly only for special symmetric linkages. The authors report a 0.05 mm median error on CFL designs. Median is not enough here: clearance is a worst-case property.
- Prop. 2 (coverage). The chord between the link-line ends, which sit at +-G/2 and depth z above the pole, is G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2). The G = 0 and z = 0 limits check out. So the denominator is correct. I also checked the claim that lowering the pole by delta adds 2 delta tan(q_req/2) to both numerator and denominator at q_req: -G(1-cos)+2z sin changes by 2 delta[tan(1-cos)+sin] = 2 delta tan. Correct. At q < q_req the denominator grows by less than the numerator, so the monotonicity also holds for the average C̄_pad. The argument treats z as constant, though, and the paper should say so.
- Prop. 3 / Eq. (6). Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and |1 - e^{iq}| = 2 sin(q/2). Correct. A nice consequence the authors could state outright: Delta c does not depend on which distal-face point is used, provided both designs use the same body point. That is what justifies "c stands for any such point".
- Eq. (7). The pole coordinates in the face frame and the link frame differ by t n. The triangle inequality gives |Delta p_face| + |Delta p_link| >= |Delta t|, and evaluating at q_req gives Eq. (7). Correct, provided the face is parallel to the link line.
- Corollary 1. Correct. Equality needs q_req <= pi so that sin(q/2) peaks at q_req; that holds for 150 deg but should be stated. I checked the rolling-joint pole: P = -i r tan(q/4), so z = r tan(q/4). Correct. Fig. 4 is consistent with the bound: 8.49, 10.39 and 11.6 mm at 6 mm for 90, 120 and 150 deg. The 10-51% figures check out too (0.35/3.46 and 4.31/8.49).

Problems I found:
1. The Sec. IV-B explanation via Eq. (3) has the wrong sign. In Eq. (3) a longer contour (larger denominator) lowers C_pad. A fixed gap that "shortens" the contour when flexed therefore helps 2R-remote's ratio. The real cause of the 150 deg crossover is the numerator through Prop. 1: the fixed-axis gap grows by 2 tan 75 = 7.46 mm per mm of face depth, which is over 50 mm for a 7 mm pad on a 90 mm finger. As written, the explanation contradicts the paper's own equation.
2. "The CFL can [keep the contact Jacobian]" overstates the result. The CFL reduces Delta_contact to 10-51% of the bound; it does not keep it. More fundamentally, the target motion (the reference coupler motion conjugated by a palmar translation of Delta h) is generated by a four-bar whose pivots are the reference pivots translated by Delta h. A generic four-bar coupler motion has only its two moving pivots on exact circles. So exact preservation at a fixed mount depth needs pivots below where they can physically go. The Delta_contact floor for a four-bar is structural, not an optimiser artefact. The paper should state this, and ideally give a four-bar-specific bound, instead of comparing only to the fixed-axis bound.
3. "Contact Jacobian" is used more broadly than what is measured. Only the PIP column in F_p is kept. The MCP column changes (the text admits this), and Delta_contact is a sup-norm on position. Keeping c(q) for all q does imply keeping J, but a small Delta_contact gives no bound on the Jacobian error beyond what follows by differentiating. The title promises more than the paper delivers.
4. Synthesis method. Study 2 is a motion-generation problem (match a pole curve shifted by Delta h). It is solved with NSGA-II over all parameters, with no use of finite-position theory, although the paper cites Bottema and Roth. Burmester-type exact matching at 4-5 precision poses (or a least-squares pole-curve fit) would show what is achievable and would make the "CFL can" claim principled.
5. H1 is close to trivially true. The comparison is against 2R-DD, a servo sitting in the joint. The informative comparison is with 2R-remote, against which the CFL loses at 90 deg and at small thicknesses at 120 deg. C1 is phrased as remote actuation plus polycentric joint, but the hypothesis tests only the weaker contrast.
6. Study 3. Much of the error under a reused command comes from over-closure: U presses 3.3x harder, and the friction-limited load scales with normal force. Under a reused torque the error falls to 3-40%, and matched force gives 3-7%. The kinematic message survives the post hoc partial correlation, but the main mechanism is grasp-force mismatch through a position-controlled grasp. The sample is also designed: R, U and set-Delta_contact families, with Eq. (7) coupling Delta_skel and Delta_contact. The correlations therefore partly reflect how the sample was chosen. n is small (15, 14, 6 hands), and the CIs are wide ([0.41, 1.00]).
7. Some inconsistencies. Delta_contact for 2R-DD and RCJ includes an offset in c0 ("start apart"), which violates the same-c0 premise of Prop. 3 / Cor. 1. Once the offset is removed, the CFL is least in only 7 of 9 cells. Units are mixed ("0.085-0.096 of C̄_pad" next to "points"). For q_req = 90 deg, Q_eval collapses to a single pose. The Stage-2 C1 test ("fails if an exchange needs the articulation taken apart") seems to conflict with exchanging CFL links. It also tests exchange effort, whereas H1 is about coverage.
8. Hardware feasibility. Several design differences are around 1 mm (CFL Delta_contact at 5-7 mm), but there is no tolerance or sensitivity analysis of the crossed four-bar's pole to link-length and pin-clearance errors. The Wilcoxon test over placements and directions treats within-hand repeats as independent when n = 1 redesign pair.

Strengths: the unifying pole view is correct and elegant. Prop. 1's mirror argument is clean. Eq. (6)-(8) give a closed-form, checkable bound that the data respect (2R-remote meets it exactly). The hypotheses have explicit failure criteria. Post hoc analyses are labelled as such. The Stage-2 protocol is concrete. For Stage 1 the methods are essentially complete.

Verdict: a solid Stage-1 abstract with mostly correct theory and a clear protocol. It is overstated in places ("keep the Jacobian", "the CFL can") and has one explanatory sign error. Borderline-positive: 6, Accept for Stage 1, conditional on fixing the C2 framing.
