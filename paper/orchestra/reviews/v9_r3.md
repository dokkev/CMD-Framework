THOUGHT:

My background is mechanism design and planar kinematics, so I checked the lemma, the three propositions, Eq. (7) and the corollary step by step before weighing the experiments.

Checking the theory.

- Lemma 1. Correct and classical. (I - R_q)^{-1} = (1/2)(I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}), so P = (1/2)(b + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2} b). This matches the Bottema-Roth displacement pole. I worked an RCJ with r = 1 and q = 90 deg by hand. It gives P = (0, tan 22.5 deg), which agrees with the claim z = r tan(q/4) used in the corollary proof. One sentence is imprecise: "The finite pole equals the instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis." The finite pole P(q) of the displacement 0 -> q tends to the instantaneous centre at q = 0 as q -> 0, and for a moving-centre joint it can coincide with the instantaneous centre at q at isolated angles. The intended statement is "for all q".
- Proposition 1 (clearance). The mirror argument is neat and correct. Under S1 and S2, the distal pad at q = 0 is the mirror image of the proximal pad in the vertical line through the pole. Composing with the rotation by q gives a reflection in the line through P rotated by q/2. A convex set and its reflection meet iff the set meets the line, because points on the line are fixed. With the pad below the pole, the deep face corner is binding, which gives G >= 2 (t - z)_+ tan(q/2). The numbers check: 5 tan 60 deg = 8.66 mm, 9 tan 60 deg = 15.6 mm, 4 tan 60 deg = 6.93 mm, and 2 tan 60 deg = 3.46 mm per mm. Two caveats:
  - The mirror-image step needs equal pad lengths, or half-infinite pads. Only the corner matters, so this is harmless but should be said.
  - S2 asks the joint-side corners to stay equidistant in x from a pole that moves with q. For a four-bar, P(q) generally moves in x as well as in depth. S2 can then hold at most at one q, so Prop. 1 is exact for a fixed axis but only an approximation for the CFL. The 0.05 mm median agreement is reassuring, but the maximum deviation should be reported, and the proposition should be stated as exact only for z-only pole motion.
- Proposition 2 (coverage). I re-derived it. The proximal end is (-G/2, 0) and the distal start is its reflection in the mirror line. Twice the distance to the line is G cos(q/2) + 2 z sin(q/2), which gives the stated denominator. The numerator follows from T1 = T2 under the mirror symmetry, since the mirror line is the bisector of the V. The text then claims that lowering the pole raises C_pad until the pole reaches the face, so the best joint turns about a point on the face. This is not derived in the paper. I checked it: with G = G_sym active at q_req, dN/dz = 2T and dD/dz = 2T(1 - cos) + 2 sin <= 2T, so the sign is positive while z < t, and negative after. The claim is right, but it should be shown in one line. It also has an awkward consequence. The optimal "joint" is a hinge whose pole sits on the face for all q, which is a fixed pin at face depth. The case for a virtual pole therefore rests entirely on the packaging statement "a physical pin must sit inside the phalanx, above the pad's mounting surface". That statement is asserted, not proven. A clevis or lateral lugs flanking the pad can carry a pin at face depth, as door-hinge style knuckles do. So can a pin offset palmar from the link line, which the model excludes by fixing the axes collinear at q = 0. The paper should defend this assumption, or state it explicitly as a design constraint, because the whole CFL argument hinges on it.
- Proposition 3. Correct: Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and ||(I - R_q) v|| = 2 sin(q/2) ||v||. Fig. 4d (2R-remote lying exactly on 2 sin(q/2)*6) is a nice numerical confirmation.
- Eq. (7). Correct by the triangle inequality, since F_p and the link-line frame differ by a translation of depth t. This has a consequence for Study 3. Within one thickness, Delta_contact and Delta_skel are anti-coupled by construction. The negative Spearman of the error with Delta_skel (-0.40, -0.51) is therefore largely a geometric consequence of Eq. (7) plus the redesign sampling, not independent evidence that "the skeleton does not matter".
- Corollary 1. Correct, including equality when the axis stays put along the face (Delta_contact is max over q of 2 sin(q/2)|Delta p|, increasing on [0, pi]). It is close to a restatement of Prop. 3 with p = axis. Calling 2 sin(q_req/2)|Delta h - Delta d| a "bound" is fair only under the packaging assumption above. Without it, recessing or offsetting the pin reaches zero. Fig. 4 also labels the dotted line 2 sin(q/2) Delta h, while 2R-DD sits below it because Delta d != 0. The line is then a reference, not a bound, for that architecture.
- Terminology. J = d/dq [c; phi] = [c'; 1] is the derivative of a body point of the distal phalanx in the proximal-face frame, with respect to the relative angle. Calling it "the PIP column of the contact Jacobian" stretches the usual meaning (map from actuated joint rates to contact velocities, in a world or palm frame). "The rotation part is the same on every design" holds by the choice of q as the relative rotation, and should be stated as a convention.

The redesign problem is a motion-generation problem. Its target is the reference's relative motion re-expressed relative to a deeper face. Classical finite-position synthesis (Burmester theory) says a four-bar can match five positions exactly. The paper uses GA/NSGA-II and never explains why Delta_contact stays at 0.35-4.31 mm. In particular, it does not say whether the residual comes from the four-bar's limits or from the collision, transmission-angle and coverage constraints. Translating the reference CFL's four pivots palmar by Delta h would give Delta_contact = 0 exactly, so the authors should say which constraint blocks that. This is the crux of "why a CFL". The synthesis literature (McCarthy and Soh; Bottema-Roth beyond the lemma) is not cited.

Also, Delta_contact depends on c0, the "point below the fingertip". Fig. 4d shows 2R-DD and RCJ start 5-9 mm apart at q = 0 because their contact point moved along the face. RCJ's large Delta_contact at 9 mm/120 deg is mostly this q = 0 offset. Is that offset physically meaningful, or an artefact of the reference point? A pure shift along the face could perhaps be absorbed by the object placement. The comparison of architectures in Study 2 may be inflated against RCJ and 2R-DD by it.

Experiments (Study 3). The central empirical claim is that the error follows Delta_contact "not the skeleton". Object placement is defined relative to the proximal pad face ("an object resting on a pad can only be placed relative to that pad's face"). The grid is "fixed independently of any hand", but it must also be face-referenced, otherwise thicker pads would interpenetrate the object. Once the object is located in the face frame, the face-frame motion mismatch is by construction the relevant geometric quantity. The result is then close to circular, though the quantification is still useful. In real interface studies the object is located by the arm or wrist, i.e. relative to the palm and MCP. The MCP column is explicitly not kept, and the MCP is held at 0 in all experiments. A skeleton-referenced placement or approach protocol is the obvious counter-test and is missing.

The statistics also do not back the phrase "only predictor":
- n = 14-18 designed (not sampled) redesigns.
- The criterion is "95% CI excludes zero" over five candidates, with no correction for multiple candidates and no test of the difference between dependent correlations.
- Under the torque protocol, thickness (0.50) and coverage (-0.52) are not far from Delta_contact (0.75).
- The shape result (rho = 0.94) uses six hands, yet the abstract quotes the range 0.75-0.94.

Practical significance. R still errs 21% and 41% at 7 and 9 mm under the reused command. Matching contact force gives 3-7%, and closing from the hand's own touch gives 20%. So the proposed redesign reduces the confound but does not remove it, and its residual is comparable to cheap protocol fixes. The authors argue those fixes rely on sensing that the interface changes, but joint torque or current sensing is not changed by the pad. A stated tolerance and a comparison against the R residual would clarify what "comparable" means.

The error metric is also a proxy. It measures the effect of a third change (friction 1.3 -> 2.0) on two hands, not the interface comparison itself (3 vs 9 mm). In simulation the true friction effect is thickness-invariant if the geometry matches. On real compliant pads it will not be, which the authors acknowledge, but the link between this proxy and the confound in an A-vs-B interface study should be argued.

Study 1. C2 is stated against 2R-DD only. 2R-remote beats the CFL at 90 deg and for thin pads at 120 deg. Switching to "pad length over the fixed L_f" to claim a lead in every condition beyond 3 mm reads as post hoc. Mechanical advantage is unconstrained, which matters for a CFL driven through a crank.

Stage-1 fit. Hypotheses with stated failure conditions, a clear Stage-2 plan, and a falsifiable structure. This is a good fit for the venue. The math is correct. The writing is compact but dense, with many coined symbols (x0, G, L_vp, S1/S2, O/R/U) and a very long title. "Same Hand" in the title overclaims, since R requires new CFL links.

Overall: an interesting, well-posed question with correct but mostly classical kinematics. The decisive packaging assumption is unproven, and the empirical test is partly built into its own frame choice. I lean to reject, but could be persuaded given the Stage-1 leniency on experiments.
