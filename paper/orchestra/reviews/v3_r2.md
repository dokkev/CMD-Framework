THOUGHT:

My background is robotic manipulation and contact modelling, so I read the paper mainly for the simulation study (Sec. V, Figs. 1d and 5) and its statistics. I also check whether the kinematic results (Secs. III and IV) support the framing.

What the paper claims. Thicker contact interfaces force a finger redesign. The redesign should keep the "contact-surface motion" c(q), meaning the distal-face point's path in the proximal-phalanx frame, which is the PIP column of the contact Jacobian. It should not try to keep the skeleton (fingertip path). A fixed-axis joint cannot keep c(q) when the pads thicken, and Corollary 1 gives a closed-form bound. A crossed four-bar (CFL) can keep it partially. In simulation, the error in a measured friction effect follows Delta_contact (rho = 0.83, n = 15) and not Delta_skel.

Intuitions on the theory. Lemma 1 and the mirror-image property are classical planar kinematics (finite pole, Burmester). The authors cite Bottema & Roth and Murray et al. accordingly. Props. 1-3 and Cor. 1 are short consequences of these facts. They are correct as far as I can check. Eq. (6) is independent of c0 because Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, which is neat. The useful part is the framing: put the pole relative to the face, not the link line, and read off the pad-clearance and coverage laws. That is a reasonable design insight. It is not a deep theoretical contribution. There is one logic slip. The text says "Since Delta_contact <= ||Delta c(0)|| + int ||Delta J|| dq, keeping c from the same pose at extension is keeping J." The inequality shows the converse (keeping J and c(0) keeps c). A small max-norm Delta_contact does not imply a small Delta J pointwise. This matters because the paper's main framing is "keep the contact Jacobian".

Intuitions on Study 1/2. Coverage comparisons rest on a GA/NSGA-II search with three seeds. CFL runs spread by 1.3-3.4 points, and CFL got an extra neighbourhood search worth "up to 2.1 points". The lowest CFL vs. 2R-DD margin (2.4 points) is therefore about the size of the search noise and the search bias. The authors disclose this honestly, but the claim "CFL exceeds 2R-DD in all 10 conditions" should be qualified. Study 2: 2R-DD and RCJ go below the fixed-axis "bound" by changing face depth or contact location (Fig. 4d starts them at 5-9 mm at q = 0). Their Delta_contact is therefore dominated by Delta c(0) and is not comparable to CFL's. The claim "CFL changes the contact-surface motion least among physical joints in every cell" holds. The 10-51% of bound ratio is a fair summary, but at 9 mm the preservation is weak (2.3-4.3 mm). The authors say so.

Main concerns on Study 3 (the claim that motivates everything).

1. The protocol builds in the result. The cylinder is placed "where the reference O touches it with both pads at q0". The same flexion command is then sent to every hand at a fixed joint stiffness K0. Under position/impedance control with near-rigid contact, a hand whose distal face sits closer to the cylinder at q0 + 5 deg penetrates more and squeezes harder. A hand whose face sits farther squeezes less, or loses the object (the three failures are exactly the hands whose face "retreats 1.5-2.6 mm"). The friction-limited load is roughly proportional to mu times the normal force. So Delta(H) / Delta(O) is roughly N_H / N_O, and E is roughly |N_H / N_O - 1|. The study then mostly measures grip-force change caused by face offset at one pose, with the object placed in O's frame. The authors' own numbers confirm this. The local face offset at the grasp predicts E better than Delta_contact (rho = 0.92 vs 0.83). Reusing torque (equal force) collapses E to 3-40%, and matching contact force leaves 3-7%. That Delta_contact correlates with this offset is close to definitional, because Delta_contact is a max-over-q bound on the same face displacement. Delta_skel is measured at the fingertip, which the cylinder does not touch, so its weakness as a predictor is unsurprising. The comparison therefore reads partly as a strawman. A fairer protocol would place the object per hand (where each hand touches it at q0, as an experimenter would). Then one could ask whether Delta_contact still predicts the residual error, for example through contact location along the face and grasp-normal direction.

2. Rigid contact removes the interface property that motivates thick pads. Thick compliant pads matter because of their compliance, contact-patch growth and pressure distribution (soft-finger models, e.g. power-law contact). In Study 3 the pads are rigid. Thickness changes only geometry, and the "interface change" is a friction coefficient. Friction is the interface property whose measured effect is most directly a function of normal force, so it is the property most exposed to the force confound above. The crowned-pad test is a step towards geometry, but the authors admit it cannot separate Delta_contact from Delta_skel. Without a compliant contact model (e.g. hydroelastic, FEM or at least a calibrated soft-contact model per thickness), we do not know whether the conclusion survives the physics that real interface studies measure. The robustness sweep over the solver's contact stiffness helps, but it varies solver regularisation and not pad material.

3. Sample design and pseudo-replication.
   - n = 15 hands, and the hands are not a random sample. Besides R and U, the redesigns were "chosen on design metrics so that Delta_contact, Delta_skel and C_pad vary apart". The correlation structure among predictors is then the authors' choice. The marginal Delta_skel rho = -0.40 (negative!) suggests that the chosen set makes Delta_skel and Delta_contact anti-correlated. Then "Delta_skel does not predict E" is partly a property of the selection. Please report the Delta_contact-Delta_skel correlation in the set, the design-selection rule, and a scatter of E against Delta_skel.
   - "It does so at each thickness": each thickness has 4-6 hands. At n = 5 a Spearman test reaches one-sided p < 0.05 only at rho = 1. Please give the per-thickness rho values and n, or drop the claim.
   - The robustness Wilcoxon "pair by pair, p <= 3e-4" can only reach that p-value if the pairs are placements x directions x perturbations, not hands. There are only three R/U pairs (one per thickness). Those observations are nested within three hand pairs and are not independent. A mixed-effects model, or a test at the hand level, is needed.
   - Partial correlations (0.88; -0.18) and a standardized thickness coefficient (+0.08) are reported from n = 15 with no confidence intervals. The -0.18 partial is computed "on a symmetric log error", a different outcome transform from every other analysis. Why the switch? With many correlations reported (marginal, partial, per thickness, local offset, torque reuse, shape), there is a forking-paths concern. "All gates were fixed before the runs" is good, but please say which analyses were pre-specified.
   - Bootstrap CIs on rho, resampling hands, would be more informative than p-values.

4. Noise floor and measurement resolution. Delta(O) is only 1.2-2.7 N. E is a relative error of a difference, which amplifies any discretisation of the limit load (load ramp rate, release criterion, solver substeps). R's 3% error at 5 mm may be within numerical noise. Please report the error of O against itself under perturbed placement or solver settings (a null redesign). Also say how "the grasp gives way" is detected.

5. Inconsistencies and missing setup details.
   - Fig. 1d gives p = 0.0001. The text gives p = 2 x 10^-4.
   - Fig. 5a gives n = 16 for torque reuse vs 15 for position reuse. Which hand is added, and why do the other two still fail?
   - Fig. 5b shows U at K0 at about 118%, the text gives U at 52-124% across thicknesses, and Robustness says "U's error moves between 127 and 184%". Which U, at which thickness, is used in Fig. 5b and in the robustness test?
   - Fig. 1 caption: U presses 3.9x harder (17.4 vs 4.5 N), while the text gives 3.3x as the median. Fine, but say Fig. 1 is one placement.
   - Grasp configuration is unclear. "Three fingers" but a cylinder "moving freely in the sagittal plane" with the MCP held. Is the cylinder held between the proximal and distal pads of each finger (a hook/envelope grasp against gravity)? Is there an opposing surface? Which simulator (Newton or MuJoCo-Warp) produced the reported numbers, and do they agree?
   - Same joint stiffness K0 on every hand removes the transmission/MA differences (-7% to +16%) that a real servo would show. That is a favourable modelling choice and should be stated as such.

6. Stage-1 requirement. The venue asks that hypotheses and methodology be fully fleshed out and that the insights under negative results be described. The physical stage (C5) gets two sentences. It does not say which interfaces, how face motion will be tracked, what the measured interface effect is, what result would falsify the claim, or what is learned if Delta_contact does not predict the physical error. This is the weakest part with respect to the call.

Desired outcome. The paper is careful about scope ("does not remove confounding", "partial preservation"), and the CFL/pole framing is a useful design lens. But the central empirical claim (C4) rests on a protocol that ties the outcome to face offset by construction, on rigid contact, and on 15 author-selected hands with pseudo-replicated tests. I lean to reject in its current form. I could be persuaded if the authors (i) add a per-hand object-placement protocol, (ii) add compliant contact, (iii) report hand-level statistics with CIs and the design-selection rule, and (iv) write out the physical-stage protocol with falsification criteria.
