ASSESSMENT NOTES:

Hypotheses as I read them (Sec. IV-A, stated as pre-registered):
- H1: When a finger is redesigned for a thicker interface and the original hand's impedance setpoint is reused, the error the redesign adds to a measured interface effect (e_H, Eq. 9) grows with the change in contact-surface motion (Δ^prox_distal), not with the change in skeleton/fingertip path (Δ_skel).
- H2: This is not just a preload change. Δ^prox_distal still predicts the error once contact force is matched.
- H3: A policy learned on O keeps the most performance on the redesign that preserves the motion its task uses.
- There is also an analytic claim (Sec. III-A, Eqs. 1–4): a pole near the contact surfaces saves room, a fixed axis cannot keep contact motion when the surfaces deepen, and no redesign keeps both contact and skeleton motion (Eq. 3).

Evidence for each:
- Analytic part: Eq. 2 is correct. c = R c0 + (I−R)p, so Δc = (I−R)Δp, and its norm is 2 sin(q/2)‖Δp‖ for a shared c0. Eq. 3 and Eq. 4 follow under the stated assumptions (same initial contact point, MCP fixed so F_prox is a translation of F_base). The paper's own Δ definitions include q = 0 and allow c0 to move, so the clean identity does not apply directly to the measured quantities. The bound is a lower bound only for the same-c0 case, which Fig. 3(c) acknowledges.
- Design comparison (Fig. 3): the CFL covers more than at-joint in all 10 feasible conditions and keeps contact motion best in 9/9. This is credible, but it is optimization-in-simulation over a parameterization chosen by the authors.
- H1: the evidence is strong for a simulation study. It includes four Isaac Lab tests (Holm-corrected), partial ρ 0.86–0.91 with skeleton fixed, ρ = 0.94 over 27 grid redesigns, Drake replication under point and hydroelastic contact, and Table I across interface changes and commands. Weaker points:
  - In the dial and pivot, ρ is 0.43–0.53.
  - Under torque, force or touch commands, every redesign's error shrinks, which narrows the claim to position/impedance-setpoint reuse.
  - Partly by construction: objects are placed relative to the proximal pad, and the object is touched only through the pads, so contact-surface motion in F_prox is the natural predictor. Sec. IV-B2 shows the "right" frame changes when the object is palm-fixed. So the substantive finding is closer to "the skeleton is the wrong invariant" than "Δ^prox_distal is the right one".
- H2: the evidence is thin and only indirect. It rests on a partial ρ = 0.80 holding force difference fixed under an equal reused torque, and on re-referencing reducing the Drake error from 42% to 14% with the remainder still following Δ^prox. There is no direct force-matched setpoint experiment for the main pull-out task.
- H3: the weakest support.
  - Two training seeds, three tasks.
  - In the relay dial, Skeleton keeps the most and CoDe the least. The paper reinterprets this as "the motion its task uses" being the tip path and declares fingertip-end contact "outside our claim".
  - The cube result uses a third, task-specific metric (proximal-pad shift against the palm, ρ = −0.98 over 14 redesigns).
  - As stated, "the motion its task uses" is chosen after the fact, so H3 is hard to falsify. A fixed, pre-stated task→metric mapping is needed.

What the scores rest on:
- Value 3: the question "which kinematic invariant must a redesign keep so commands and policies still mean the same thing" is clean, general and timely given cross-embodiment and policy-reuse work. The pole analysis gives it a principled basis. It is held back from 4 because:
  - the scope is narrow (planar PIP only, MCP fixed, servo hands with impedance setpoints);
  - the result is partly intuitive once stated;
  - the paper's motivating interfaces (compliant, tactile) are never modeled as deforming.
- Feasibility 2:
  - Everything is simulated, and no interface deforms, although compliant and tactile pads are the motivation. Contact migration within a soft pad could change which surface motion matters.
  - H2 and H3 are under-supported, and H3's falsification criterion is flexible.
  - The hardware plan is concrete but small (three fingers, two thicknesses, two tasks).
  - Against submissions arriving with hardware and many tasks, the evidence is a careful but simulation-only correlational study.
