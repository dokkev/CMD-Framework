ASSESSMENT NOTES:
The paper claims that when a robot finger is redesigned for a different contact interface (a thicker or more compliant pad), the redesign should keep the motion of the contact surfaces, meaning their contact Jacobian in the proximal-phalanx frame. It argues the redesign should not keep the skeleton (joint axes and fingertip path). It supports this with three claims.

(i) A finite-displacement pole analysis. Eq. (3) gives ‖Δc‖ = 2 sin(q/2)‖Δp‖. Eq. (4) gives the trade-off Δ_contact + Δ_skel ≥ 2 sin(q_req/2)|Δt|. Eq. (5) bounds a fixed axis by Δ_contact ≥ 2 sin(q_req/2)|Δt|. The algebra is correct and elegant: Δc = (I−R_q)Δp and |I−R_q| = 2 sin(q/2) in the plane. It is also self-consistent with the reported numbers. For 3→7 mm at 120°, Fig. 4 gives Skeleton 6.6+0.3 = 6.9 mm ≈ 2 sin 60°·4 mm and Coverage 3.9+3.4 = 7.3 mm. However, the "impossibility" depends on Δt, which is itself a design variable ("a redesign that raises its pivots lets the surfaces deepen less"). So "no redesign keeps both" is weaker than the abstract and contributions suggest.

(ii) The crossed four-bar (CoDe) gives more room for pads than a servo-at-joint finger (+2.4–14.6 pp coverage in 10 feasible conditions; Fig. 3). This is a kinematic optimization result. It is plausible but modest, and there is no physical validation of the CAD.

(iii) In simulation, the error a redesign introduces into a measured pad effect tracks Δ_contact and not Δ_skel. The support is ρ = 0.94 (pre-registered) and partial ρ 0.86–0.91 with Δ_skel held fixed over 18 redesigns. This holds across two simulators, two contact models and several commands (Table I), with a force-controlled check (partial ρ 0.80). Learned policies transfer according to where the task touches the finger (Fig. 5). The statistics are careful for a simulation study: pre-registration, Holm correction, partial correlation, a noise floor (O vs O ≤ 6%). That is the paper's strongest asset.

External validity is the main problem. The motivating case is thick, compliant pads, yet "Neither contact model deforms a pad". Compliance is modelled as a softer point contact or a thicker hydroelastic layer. The Limitations section admits that a deforming pad also moves the contact point, which is exactly the quantity the thesis is about. There is no hardware. The designs are optimized with a planar finger and the MCP fixed, but they are tested in full-hand tasks.

Clarity is the second problem. Many design sets appear (18 correlation, 27 grid, 16 Drake, 14 policy redesigns) and are never put side by side. Naming changes between figures and text ("Conventional" vs "Coverage"). Fig. 1(d), Table I and the text use different statistics without saying so. The pole result and the empirical correlation together are a nice, somewhat original insight for interface research. On the current evidence it is a borderline contribution, so I lean reject but could be persuaded.

Presentation and clarity items:
1. Unify naming. Fig. 1(c) and 1(d) say "Conventional", while Sec. IV, Figs. 4-5 and Table I say "Coverage". Use "Coverage" everywhere, or say in the Fig. 1 caption that they are the same hand. Right now the caption only says so in a parenthetical.
2. Define Δ_MCP before using it. It appears in Sec. III-A ("(Δ_MCP, Eq. (7))") but is defined in Sec. III-C. Either move Eq. (7) earlier or state Δ_MCP in words at first use.
3. Add a small table (or a sentence in Sec. IV-A) listing every design population: the 18-redesign correlation set, 27 grid, 16 Drake, 14 policy redesigns, and the named CoDe/Coverage/Skeleton. For each, give size, thicknesses, Δ_contact/Δ_skel ranges and the simulators and tasks it is used in.
4. Table I: explain the blank cells, state the estimator (median signed relative error over N placements), add N or IQR, and use bold only for the entry closer to zero (torque: Coverage 0% vs CoDe +8%).
5. Fig. 1(d): state in the caption that the bars are median load changes (not the error statistic of Table I), and give the corresponding error values so readers can match them to Table I.
6. Abstract and first contribution: qualify "no redesign whose contact surfaces deepen keeps both" and "a fixed axis ... cannot keep that motion" with "for a given surface deepening Δt" and "from the same initial contact point". Quantify "keeps most of it" (e.g. Δ_contact = 1.1 mm vs 3.9 mm).
