# Review R3 (v2) — mechanism design / kinematics perspective

THOUGHT:

## Notes (note-taking phase)

**What the paper claims.** When a finger is redesigned to carry a thicker interface, the thing to hold fixed is the relative motion of the two contact faces, c(q) in the proximal-face frame (equivalently the PIP column [c'; 1] of the contact Jacobian), and not the skeleton. The analysis uses the finite displacement pole. It gives a clearance law (Prop. 1), a coverage law (Prop. 2), a trajectory law (Prop. 3) and a fixed-axis lower bound (Cor. 1). Study 1 (H1) compares four PIP architectures on best coverage C̄_pad. Study 2 (H2) redesigns each architecture's 3 mm design for thicker pads while minimizing D_c. Study 3 (H3) checks in simulation that the error in a measured friction effect follows D_c and not D_s when commands are reused. H4 (hardware) is Stage 2.

**Checks of the mathematics (I worked these by hand).**
- Lemma 1: (I − R_q)^{-1} = ½I + ½cot(q/2)·R_{π/2}, since det(I − R_q) = 2(1 − cos q) and sin q/(1 − cos q) = cot(q/2). b = r_C(q) − R_q r_C(0) is correct. Correct, and classical.
- Prop. 3: Δc(q) = (I − R_q)Δp and ‖(I − R_q)v‖ = 2 sin(q/2)‖v‖. Correct. It needs q ≤ π for the max over q to sit at q_req, which holds here.
- Prop. 1: The corner condition G/2 ≥ (t − z)tan(q/2) gives Eq. (2). The fixed-axis slopes 2tan45° = 2.00, 2tan60° = 3.46 and 2tan75° = 7.46 check out. Fig. 4 checks out: 5tan60° = 8.66, 9tan60° = 15.6, 4tan60° = 6.93. The step "a convex set and its mirror are disjoint iff it lies in one half-plane" holds, because points on the mirror line are fixed by the reflection. S2 ("corners stay equidistant from the pole in x for all q") is strong for a four-bar whose pole drifts. The authors say the optima satisfy S1–S2 only approximately. Good.
- Prop. 2: I re-derived the phalanx-end distance and got |G cos(q/2) ∓ 2z sin(q/2)| (the sign is the sense convention), so the law is right. It silently assumes the pads' joint-side ends coincide with the phalanx ends (G = L_vp). Otherwise L_f(q) is not L_f − G(1 − cos) + 2z sin. This should be stated.
- RCJ pole: For two equal rollers whose centres lie on the link line, I computed the finite pole at depth r·tan(q/4) on the perpendicular through the q = 0 contact. This matches the text. (If the roller centres were placed normal to the link line, the pole would lie on the link line instead. The geometry should be stated.)
- **Cor. 1 has a hidden premise.** The proof uses Eq. (4), which requires the same c_0. The corollary says "wherever the new axis is placed" and the proof handles the pole shifting along the face. It does not handle a shift of c_0, the face point under the fingertip. Fig. 3d shows exactly that for 2R-DD and RCJ ("start apart"). With Δc(0) = a (along the face) and a normal pole shift δ: ‖Δc(q)‖² = 2δ²(1 − cos q) + a² + 2aδ sin q. At q_req = 120° with a = −0.866δ, max_q ‖Δc‖ = 1.5δ < 2 sin60°·δ = 1.73δ. So two fixed-axis designs can beat Eq. (5) by moving c_0, not only by changing mount depth. Two consequences:
  - The "bound" is a bound only under pinned c_0, and this premise belongs in the statement.
  - Part of why 2R-DD and RCJ fall below the dotted line may be c_0 drift, not Δd.
  - Separately, "fixed roller radius" is a real restriction. If r is free in Study 2, an RCJ can move its pole by Δr·tan(q/4), so it is a (weakly) pole-moving joint. That conflicts with the H2 framing "only a joint that moves its pole".
- Terminology: "a design differs only by c', the pole of the relative motion". c' is a velocity. Together with c it determines the instantaneous centre, but it is not the pole. Also, J denotes both the Jacobian and R_{π/2}. τ_i is defined and never used. "D_s is the same for the fingertip" is unclear (same functional of r_C?).
- D_c inequality: ‖Δc(q)‖ ≤ ‖Δc(0)‖ + ∫‖ΔJ‖. Fine. One rigid-body point plus φ = q fixes the whole distal-face pose in F_p, so a single point c is enough. Good choice, and worth one sentence saying so.

**Checks of the numbers.** Study 1: CFL losses from 3 to 9 mm are 8.5/8.6/9.6 points and 2R-remote's are 10.7/18.2. At 5 mm, 90° → 150° costs CFL 3.9 and 2R-remote 21.6. All match Fig. 2. Study 2 percentages: 0.35/3.46 = 10% and 4.31/8.49 = 51%. Consistent. Spearman ρ = 0.83 with n = 15 gives t ≈ 5.4, p ≈ 1.3e-4. Fine, but report it as 1e-4 to 2e-4, not "10^-4".

**Internal inconsistencies.**
- Fig. 1 caption: U presses "3.9× harder" (17.4/4.5 = 3.87). Text, Sec. V-D: "3.3× harder".
- "Hands with D_cL_f ≥ 2.9 mm cannot hold the cylinder" contradicts Figs. 1d and 5a. Those figures show held hands at 3.0, 3.1, 3.5, 3.9 and 5.1 mm. Presumably the meaning is "the three failures all have D_cL_f ≥ 2.9 mm".
- R "grips like O", yet its distal force is 3.0 N against 4.5 N (0.67×).
- Contribution (4) says 18 redesigns, but the main correlation uses 15. Fig. 5a torque reuse uses n = 16.
- At 150°, the 2R-remote designs reported as C̄_pad = 0.21 "cannot hold a cylinder", yet have non-zero coverage. This needs explaining.
- The abstract mixes "0.182 of its coverage" (absolute points? relative?) with "points" elsewhere.
- Fig. 4 is cited for "for a four-bar, z(q) and P_x(q) are set by the links", but it shows no four-bar.
- "Rigid contact" sits next to "the contact's compliance takes the interference" (MuJoCo contact is soft-constraint).

**Statistics and design concerns.**
- Study 1 search noise vs. claimed margins. CFL seeds spread 1.3–3.4 points and the extra search added up to 2.1. 2R-remote leads at 90° by 1.9–4.1, and the smallest CFL-over-2R-DD margin is 2.4. The extra effort was given to CFL only ("extra effort favours CFL"). That is an asymmetric search budget in a comparison between architectures. H1's test ("losses must differ") has no noise threshold. Each architecture needs equal budgets and a margin judged against the seed spread.
- H1 is partly falsified by its own criterion. RCJ-ideal is among the best, which is the "packaging" branch. 2R-remote wins at 90°. The paper notes both, but "Fig. 2 supports H1" overstates it. H1 as phrased ("architecture bounds…, spread grows") is weakly falsifiable.
- H3 is close to mechanically expected. With Coulomb friction, the load-to-failure scales with normal force, so a multiplicative interface change (µ 1.3 → 2.0) makes E ≈ |N_H/N_O − 1|. Under a reused position command, N is set by how far the faces over-close at q_0 + 5°, which is what Δc(q_0) measures. Torque reuse removing most of the effect (3–40%), and force matching leaving 3–7%, fit this reading. So the experiment mostly shows "geometry mismatch → grip-force mismatch under position control". That is true but nearly by construction. It is weak evidence for the broader claim that keeping the whole J(q) is what interface studies need. The pending shape/compliance interface is therefore essential, not optional.
- Metric locality. D_c is a max over [0, q_req], but the experiment probes only near q_0. ‖Δc(q_0)‖ or Δc'(q_0) should be compared as competing predictors. If a local metric predicts as well, the claim about the full trajectory is not tested.
- "Not D_s" is not tested. ρ = −0.40 with n = 15 (p ≈ 0.14) is absence of evidence. The hands were chosen so that D_c, D_s and C̄_pad "vary apart", which likely makes D_s anti-correlated with D_c by selection. It needs partial correlations or a joint rank regression (E ~ D_c + D_s + h + C̄_pad), a comparison of dependent correlations (Steiger/Williams), and bootstrap CIs. "Thickness adds nothing once D_c is known" has no stated test.
- Censoring. The 3 hands that cannot hold are dropped. Treat them as censored at maximal error and recompute ρ. It would likely strengthen the result, but it must be shown.
- E has |Δ(O)| = 1.2–2.7 N in the denominator. Per-hand uncertainty appears in Fig. 5b only. Fig. 5a/1d should carry IQR bars.
- Study 2 uses NSGA-II with 2 seeds and reports the coverage end of the front. Its seed variance is not reported.

**Novelty from a mechanisms viewpoint.** The finite pole, the 2 sin(q/2) chord and pole placement by four-bars are textbook (Bottema–Roth). So are polycentric knees and four-bar finger joints. The new part is the framing: using the face-relative pole as the invariant that makes interface comparisons fair, plus the clearance/coverage laws tied to pad depth. That is a useful lens, but Props. 1–3 and Cor. 1 are short consequences of classical facts. They should be presented as such, not as contributions on par with the experiments. Related work misses the underactuated/linkage-finger kinematics literature (e.g., Birglen–Laliberté–Gosselin) and ICR/pole-trajectory synthesis for polycentric finger and exoskeleton joints. Both are directly tied to "moving the pole".

**What I need from the TODO part.** (i) E vs D_c under contact stiffness ×0.1–×10, halved timestep and a second solver, with the ranking (ρ and its CI) reported. (ii) A shape/compliance interface whose effect is not a pure normal-force multiplier, with ρ(E, D_c) and the partial ρ given D_s and h. (iii) If possible, the same at q_0 values other than 105° to test the full-trajectory claim against the local one.

**Overall judgement.** The paper is careful and honest about its negative branches (2R-remote at 90°, RCJ ideal, partial H2). The hypotheses are explicit and falsifiable, which suits Stage 1. The mathematics is correct apart from the c_0 premise in Cor. 1. The weaknesses are about claims outrunning evidence:
- an asymmetric search effort under margins near the noise;
- an H3 test that is close to tautological for a friction interface;
- an untested "not D_s" claim;
- several numeric inconsistencies;
- prose that is very dense.

As a Stage 1 abstract it is borderline. I lean reject but could be persuaded if the robustness and shape-interface results come in and the statistics are tightened.
