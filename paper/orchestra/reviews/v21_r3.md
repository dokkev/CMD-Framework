ASSESSMENT NOTES:
Perspective: mechanism kinematics. I re-derived every result in Sec. III and checked the numbers that depend on them.

Mathematics (Sec. III), checked:
- Lemma 1. (I - R_q)^{-1} = 1/(2(1 - cos q)) [[1-cos q, -sin q],[sin q, 1-cos q]] = 1/2 (I + cot(q/2) R_{pi/2}). Correct; it is the standard finite-pole result (Bottema-Roth). The sentence "the finite pole equals the instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis" is loose. The two always coincide as q -> 0. What is meant is that they coincide for all q only for a fixed axis.
- Proposition 1. With y dorsal and the proximal joint-side face corner at (-G/2, -(t - z)) relative to the pole, the bisector of (-1,0) and (cos q, -sin q) has normal (cos(q/2), -sin(q/2)). The signed distance is -G/2 cos(q/2) + (t - z) sin(q/2). This gives G >= 2(t - z) tan(q/2), so Eq. (2) is right, and the face corner, not the mounting corner, is the binding one. The "iff" relies on the distal pad being the mirror of the proximal pad. That holds exactly near the joint-side corner under S1 and S2. With unequal pad lengths, a one-line remark that the far ends cannot bind is needed. Fig. 2 checks: 5 tan 60 = 8.66, 9 tan 60 = 15.6, 4 tan 60 = 6.93. The text checks too: 2 tan 60 = 3.46 mm per mm, and tan 75 / tan 45 = 3.73 ("nearly four times").
- Proposition 2. The chord between the two phalanx ends, both at radius sqrt(G^2/4 + z^2) from the pole, is G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2). The denominator is correct. With G at its bound, d(den)/dz = 2 tan(Q)(1 - cos(q/2)) + 2 sin(q/2), which equals 2 tan(Q) at q = q_req, as claimed. At q < q_req the denominator grows less than the numerator, by 2 sin(q/2 - Q)/cos Q < 0. The "lower the pole, raise coverage" law therefore holds for the whole C-bar_pad average, not only at q_req, and the authors could say so.
- Proposition 3 / Eq. (7) / Corollary 1. Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, with ||(I - R_q) v|| = 2 sin(q/2) ||v||. Correct, and notably independent of c0, which justifies "c stands for any such point". Eq. (7) follows from the triangle inequality between the pole shifts in the link frame and in the face frame, given that c0 and r_C0 are kept. The equality condition of Corollary 1 holds because sin(q/2) is monotone on (0, 150 deg]. The RCJ claim z = r tan(q/4) checks: at q = 90 deg I get a pole depth of 0.414 r. Fig. 1(b,c) is consistent with Eq. (7) for a 4 mm depth change at 120 deg (bound 6.93 mm). U: 3.9 + 3.4 = 7.3. R: 0.9 + 7.7 = 8.6.

I found no mathematical error. The design section is compact and correct, and it gives a single quantity (the pole's depth, and its path relative to the face) that explains both C1 and C2. This is the paper's main strength for me.

Do the studies test the propositions?
- H1 / Study 1 tests the C1 claim directly: 10 of 10 feasible conditions, with a pre-stated failure rule and the worst-of-six-runs check. The pole-depth reading (2.4-7.4 mm deep, at most 1.5 mm of drift along the finger, and Eq. (2) reproducing the optimised gap to a median of 0.05 mm) ties the optimiser's output to Props. 1-2 well. One link is missing. Prop. 2 says coverage rises until the pole reaches the face, but the CFL pole stays 3.6-4.6 mm above the face. The paper does not say which constraint stops it (the transmission angle, the drift of the pole in x, link collision with the pads, or MA). That is the mechanism-design insight a reader wants.
- H2 / Study 2 compares the CFL with the fixed-axis bound at Delta d = 0. That is legitimate only because pads are mounted at the shallowest clear depth, so a fixed axis cannot recess. The paper should state this link explicitly at Corollary 1 and Fig. 4. As written, "less than the fixed-axis bound" reads as beating a lower bound that a recessed axis could reach. The Pareto check ("giving up 2 points ... stay above the CFL") does address matched coverage.
- Fig. 4: for 2R-DD and RCJ the distance ||Delta c(q)|| is nonzero at q = 0 (about 5 and 9 mm in (d)), because c0 is not kept. Delta_contact then mixes a placement offset with the pole-path difference, which is the only thing that changes the Jacobian column. Prop. 3 itself suggests a c0-free metric, max 2 sin(q/2)||Delta p(q)||. The authors should either use it or justify keeping c0 in the metric. For the RCJ, the 9 mm start offset needs explaining.
- H3 / Study 3 tests C2 with pre-registered criteria, rival candidates, partial correlations and a falsification rule. That is good practice. The causal story is, however, close to geometric interference. Under a reused q-space impedance setpoint, the grasp force is set by how far the face sits from where O's face was. The partial rho of 0.64 with the setpoint force held fixed shows that Delta_contact carries more than force, which helps. The obvious control is missing: a Delta_contact = 0 redesign (spacers). Its E would show whether the roughly 20% floor in Fig. 5 is a non-kinematic residual or comes from the MCP column or pad geometry. "Studied on its own" with a 20% error also needs its threshold argued, not just stated.
- The setpoint lives in flexion space at K0 = 5 N m/rad. A real servo realises stiffness in motor space, and through a four-bar K_q = K_s (d theta/dq)^2 varies with q and between redesigns. The statement "the transmission is not part of J" assumes the controller compensates for this. The Stage 2 physical protocol must say how.

Scope and presentation. The claims are carefully scoped: C2 holds under reused setpoints and does not hold under the torque or touch protocols, where Delta_skel predicts, and the paper says so. The prose is extremely dense. Captions carry results ("0.085-0.096 of C-bar_pad" next to "points" elsewhere, and "(0.21, 0)" is unexplained). Table I's caption is a paragraph. Most of the numbers check out, but readability suffers. As a Stage 1 abstract, the problem, hypotheses, method, falsification criteria and a Stage 2 physical protocol are all present.

Scores rest on: correct and useful kinematics, studies that mostly test the propositions, and a few gaps (the missing Delta_contact = 0 control, the c0 dependence of the metric, the unexplained optimum pole depth, and the controller-space assumption). Simulation only, with one contact model.
