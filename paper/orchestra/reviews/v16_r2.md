THOUGHT:
I read this as a contact-mechanics and experimental-methodology reviewer. The paper has two halves of different maturity. The kinematic half (Sec. III, Studies 1 and 2) is closed-form and I could check it: Lemma 1 is the standard finite pole, Eq. (6) follows from Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, the numbers quoted from Eq. (2) (3.46 mm/mm at 120 deg, 2.0 and 7.5 mm at 90 and 150 deg, 8.7 = 5 tan 60) and the contrast family's sqrt(3) Delta h are all consistent with the equations. That half is sound within its planar, single-finger-length scope. The experimental half (Study 3, H3) is where my expertise applies and where I have the most reservations.

My central question for H3 is whether it tests anything that the setup does not already imply. Under a reused flexion command with a joint stiffness K0, the normal force is K0 times the angular over-closure divided by a lever, and the over-closure is set by where the faces meet the object, which is what Delta_contact measures. The limit load at slip in a Coulomb/penalty contact model then scales with that normal force. So "error follows Delta_contact" under a reused position command is close to a geometric identity routed through a linear spring; the paper itself reports that the deviation of the distal force ratio tracks the error with rho = 0.94. The non-trivial evidence is the reused-torque protocol (rho = 0.75, CI [0.38, 0.93], n = 16) and the post hoc force-held-fixed partial (0.80). That evidence is thinner, the effect is much smaller (3-40%), and the paper also reports that matching contact force leaves only 3-7%, i.e. at the resolution of the load step. That last result cuts against the necessity of C2: a cheaper remedy than redesigning the linkage is to match force, and the interfaces of interest (tactile modules) often measure force. The paper dismisses this in half a sentence.

Second, the design of the sample of hands. The redesigns are not sampled; they are constructed (R, U, and "redesigns at set Delta_contact that keep or change the skeleton as much as possible"). Eq. (7) shows Delta_contact + Delta_skel is bounded below, so the two main contestants are anti-correlated by construction at fixed mount depth; the negative rho of the skeleton (-0.40 to -0.51) in Table I is then expected rather than informative. The partial correlations and the 45-hand grid help, and I credit them, but the grid is 7 mm only and the range of every competing predictor in the sample is the authors' choice. A rank correlation over 14-16 purpose-built hands with intervals such as [0.33, 0.99] and [0.38, 0.93] supports "Delta_contact is associated with the error in these hands", not "ranks first among five design quantities" as a general statement. The headline rho = 0.94 comes from six hands.

Third, conditioning on the outcome. The correlations are "over the hands that hold the cylinder" (15, 14, 14, 16 of 18, a different subset per setting), E is a median "over the placements and directions where both hands hold", and the fixed-axis contrast family is reported "outside the correlations" without a reason. Hands and placements that lose the grasp are presumably the worst-distorted ones, so the exclusion is informative missingness. I would want the counts and a sensitivity analysis. Similarly "all thresholds were fixed before the runs" sits next to an explicitly post hoc analysis and several successive settings (grid, soft pads, torque), with Holm applied to 25 tests when the paper reports many more tests than that.

Fourth, the contact model. The "pad" is a rigid shape with a scalar contact stiffness (1.0-2.5 kN/m) and Coulomb friction (mu 1.3 -> 2.0) in a MuJoCo-type soft-constraint solver. There is no contact patch, no shear compliance of the layer, no thickness-dependent normal/tangential coupling, no torsional friction that scales with patch size. For a 9 mm elastomer these are first-order: a thick pad shears by millimetres under tangential load, which is the same magnitude as the Delta_contact values (0.35-4.3 mm) the argument rests on. The "soft pad" setting is a stiffness scalar inversely proportional to h, which is a reasonable first step but not a deformable pad. The paper states this limitation in one sentence; I think it deserves to be scoped in the claims (abstract says "error in measured friction, stiffness and shape effects" without "in a penalty-contact rigid-body simulation").

Fifth, the Stage 2 protocol. It is concrete and has a failure criterion, which is what Stage 1 asks for. But it is one finger, two redesigns (R and U at 7 mm), and a paired Wilcoxon over placements x directions x repeats. That tests "R errs less than U" on a single pair; it cannot test H3's claim that error follows Delta_contact (a two-point comparison has no slope and no competing predictor), and the repeats within one hand are pseudo-replicates for any claim about hands. Also, in hardware O carries a real 3 mm pad and R/U real 7 mm pads, so E relative to O contains the true thickness effect that the paper says "only simulation can build" a reference for; the predicted 21 vs 124% is therefore not what the hardware will measure, though the R-vs-U ordering is still testable. The C1 hardware test (parts, time, recalibration) does not test coverage at all.

On Study 1: the coverage metric is the authors' own geometric construct (cylinder inscribed in the V) and is never tied to a grasp outcome; in Table I coverage does not predict the measurement error, and nothing else shows it matters. H1's margins start at 2.4 points with three GA seeds and no reported seed spread; CFL alone gets a refinement pass (the paper says the result holds without it, good). Mechanical advantage is unconstrained and the CFL optima land at 0.41-0.58, i.e. the CFL finger delivers roughly half the fingertip force per unit joint torque — a real cost that is reported but not weighed. Planar analysis: the four-bar's links must live somewhere in width, and "full-width pads leave no room for lugs" applies to them too.

Terminology: Delta_contact is the max positional deviation of one face point's path over q, not a difference of Jacobians. The title's "keep the contact Jacobian" is defensible via Eq. (4) (the column is the derivative of that path and the rotational part is common), but the MCP column is explicitly not kept, so "the contact Jacobian" overstates it.

What is good and should be said: falsifiable hypotheses with stated failure conditions; a real ablation (2R-remote separates remote actuation from the polycentric joint); competing predictors tested rather than only the favoured one; hand-level bootstrap rather than treating placements as independent; a second protocol (torque) designed to remove the obvious mechanism; a counterfactual reference for softening pads; robustness over joint stiffness, contact stiffness, substeps; resolution reported. For a Stage-1 abstract, where hypotheses and methods must be complete and experiments may be preliminary, this is above the bar. The negative-result insight is stated for each hypothesis. My score reflects that the theory is solid and the methodology is unusually careful for this genre, but that H3's main result is partly implied by the setup, the sample of hands is constructed, the contact model is too simple for the claim's wording, and the Stage 2 experiment is underpowered for H3. I land at 6, Accept, with the expectation that Stage 2 addresses the points above.
