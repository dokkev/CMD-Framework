ASSESSMENT NOTES:
The paper makes two claims about a servo-driven finger that carries exchangeable contact interfaces.

C1 (room for thick pads): moving the PIP servo onto the proximal phalanx and replacing the pin with a crossed four-bar (CFL) leaves more pad coverage than a servo-at-joint revolute (2R-DD) at every pad thickness and required flexion. The geometric argument is sound and well chosen. Prop. 1 shows the fixed-axis joint gap grows by 2 tan(q_req/2) per mm of face depth. A virtual pole can sink toward the face; a physical pin cannot, because it has to sit above the pad mount. Study 1 supports H1 (10/10 conditions, 2.4-14.6 points, worst-of-six CFL run beats best 2R-DD in 9/10). The 2R-remote ablation separates "remote actuation" from "polycentric joint". It is useful and honest: remote actuation alone already gives the room at 90 deg. The evidence for C1 is strong within its frame. That frame is a geometric coverage proxy (an inscribed cylinder averaged over three poses), not a grasp outcome. The real cost is mechanical advantage. A worst case of 0.41-0.58 against 1 roughly halves fingertip force on hands whose grip strength is already their weak point. The paper names the trade-off but leaves it free, so the C1 win is partly bought with force. Backlash and link compliance of a crossed four-bar under grasp load are also absent; I know from servo-chain builds that these dominate repeatability.

C2 (keep the contact Jacobian, not the skeleton): an interface effect measured on a redesigned finger is free of the redesign only if the contact-surface motion c(q) is kept. Props. 2-3 and Cor. 1 are clean and the fixed-axis bound is a useful design statement. Study 2 shows the CFL comes in at 10-51% of that bound at near-best coverage. Study 3 is the empirical core. Under a reused impedance setpoint, the interface-effect error follows Delta_contact (rho 0.80-0.86, bootstrap intervals exclude zero, Holm-corrected, pre-registered). Delta_skel and the other candidates do not. The 27-hand pre-registered grid replicates it (rho 0.94 for the soft-pad case). The reversal example (U reports +48% when the truth is -41%) makes the risk concrete. The statistics are careful for a simulation paper. Pre-registration and post hoc labels are a real plus.

My reservations on C2:
(i) Under position control at fixed stiffness, the force is set by over-closure, so "where the faces sit decides the error" is close to built into the protocol. The post hoc rho = -0.99 with the face's signed normal offset shows a simpler geometric quantity explains the sign. The partial correlations say Delta_contact subsumes it, but the gap between Delta_contact and "face offset at the grasp pose" is modest.
(ii) The claim is protocol-specific. With touch-triggered closing, Delta_contact is not significant (0.44) and the skeleton dominates (0.88). Many servo hands run current-limited position control, which is closer to a torque or touch protocol than the paper suggests. The paper scopes C2 to "the setpoints servo hands run", but readers will apply it more widely.
(iii) The contact model is point contact with a normal spring. A "thick compliant pad" therefore differs from a thin one only in stiffness and face position. Patch area, conformity and pad shear, which are why one fits a thick pad, are absent. This is acceptable for Stage 1 but limits what the 48%/-41% example says about real pads.
(iv) n is small (14-15 hands for the main setpoint tests), and the redesign population is constructed (set Delta_contact, extreme skeleton). The correlation depends on that sampling.

The Stage 2 plan is concrete and falsifiable: one CoDe finger with O, R, M, U modules, predicted errors of 21/72/124%, and a Page trend test. Whether marker-based Delta_contact and printed-link tolerances can resolve the ~1 mm design tolerance is not discussed. Presentation is dense. The table caption carries too much, the O/R/U/M labels arrive late, and Figs. 2 and 4 are hard to read at print size. The paper still has the problem statement, hypotheses, failure criteria and methods that Stage 1 asks for. My scores rest on: sound and novel geometric framing (C1 and the C2 bound), careful but protocol-bound simulation evidence for C2, and an unaddressed force cost and contact-model gap.
