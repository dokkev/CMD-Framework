ASSESSMENT NOTES:

How I read the hypotheses:
- H1: When a finger is redesigned for a thicker contact interface and the original hand's impedance setpoint is reused, the redesign's error in a measured interface effect, e_H = (D_H − D_O)/|D_O|, grows with the change in contact-surface motion seen from the proximal pad (Δ^prox_distal). It does not grow with the change in skeleton or fingertip path (Δ_skel).
- H2: This dependence is not only a change of preload. Δ^prox_distal still predicts the error once contact force is matched.
- H3: A policy learned on O keeps the most performance on the redesign that preserves the motion its task actually uses (pad-relative, tip path, or proximal pad relative to the palm).
- Design-side claims: (a) a pole analysis (Eqs. 1–4) shows that a fixed axis cannot keep contact motion when the surfaces deepen, and that contact motion and skeleton cannot both be kept (Eq. 3); (b) a crossed four-bar linkage (CFL) at the PIP covers more than at-joint, remote, and rolling-contact-joint (RCJ) alternatives and keeps contact motion best.

Evidence for each:
- Pole analysis: clean and correct as far as I can check. Eq. 2 follows from (I − R_q)Δp, and Eq. 3 is a triangle inequality on a pure translation Δt. It is elementary but useful framing, and the 3.46 mm/mm figure checks out (2 tan 60°).
- H1: Isaac Lab (MuJoCo-Warp, point contact) pull-out on a full hand.
  - Four tests, Holm-corrected; partial ρ 0.86–0.91 with skeleton held; ρ = 0.94 over 27 "pre-registered" grid redesigns.
  - Drake repeats with point and hydroelastic contact across four tasks (ρ 0.93/0.91 for hold/acquire, only 0.43–0.53 for dial/pivot).
  - The sign-reversal anecdote (−41% on O vs. +48% on Coverage) is the most compelling result in the paper.
- H2: partial ρ = 0.80 under an equal reused torque. Re-referencing the setpoint cuts the Drake error from 42% to 14%, and the remainder "still follows" Δ^prox_distal (no statistic given).
- H3: three PPO tasks with two training seeds each, 7 mm redesigns only. Results are mixed:
  - Finger walk favors CoDe.
  - Relay dial favors Skeleton, and the authors place it "outside our claim".
  - Cube rotation favors CoDe via a palm-relative metric with ρ = −0.98 over 14 redesigns. That metric appears to have been chosen after the fact.

What my scores rest on:
- Value 3. The question "what must stay invariant when a hand is redesigned" bears on interface benchmarking and on policy and command reuse across hardware revisions, and it is clearly falsifiable. Three things hold it below 4:
  - Under a position-based impedance command with rigid contact, H1 is close to expected by construction: force is K(q_target − q), and q at contact is set by where the surfaces are.
  - The scenario (reusing an unrecalibrated setpoint across a redesign) is narrow.
  - The "original hand" is itself a CFL, not the servo-at-joint hand that motivates the paper.
- Feasibility 2. All evidence is simulated with rigid or hydroelastic contact, and no interface deforms, although the motivating interfaces are compliant, gel, and tactile. A "compliant" interface is a stiffness parameter. The correlation sets are designed families, so ρ partly reflects how they were sampled. H3 rests on two seeds, includes one counterexample, and redefines "the motion the task uses" per task after the result. CoDe's coverage cost is never quantified in the experiment. The hardware plan is a sentence. Against submissions that arrive with hardware and many tasks, the evidence is thin, though the methodology is sound and the pre-registration is a real strength.
- Recommendation: invite to rebuttal, borderline. The hypothesis and test design are explicit enough to support an Extension Charter.
