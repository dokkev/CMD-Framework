THOUGHT:
I read this as a mechanism-kinematics reviewer and re-derived every numbered statement in Sec. III by hand before looking at the studies.

Mathematics (all check out).
- Lemma 1. With J = R_{pi/2}, (I - R_q)(I + cot(q/2) J)/2 = I, because (1 - cos q) cot(q/2) = sin q and (1 - cos q) + sin^2 q/(1 - cos q) = 2. The closed form is right. The remark that the finite pole equals the instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis is also right: from c = P + R(c0 - P), the instantaneous centre is p_hat = P - J^{-1}(I - R)P', which equals P for q != 0 only if P' = 0.
- Proposition 1. Under S1 and S2 the displacement is a reflection in the line through the pole at half-angle q/2. For a point at height u above the pole and G/2 from it in x, the distance to its image is G cos(q/2) + 2u sin(q/2). With u = -(d + h - z) for the face corner, non-negativity gives G >= 2(d + h - z) tan(q/2), which is Eq. (2). The face corner is the extremal one for q < 180 deg.
- Fig. 2 numbers: 5 tan 60 = 8.66, 9 tan 60 = 15.59, 4 tan 60 = 6.93; pad lengths 32/25/34 mm are consistent with these to rounding. 2 tan 60 = 3.46 mm per mm is right, and tan 75 / tan 45 = 3.73 matches "nearly four times".
- Proposition 2. Numerator follows from the definition. Phalanx ends at u = +z give a separation G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2) (I verified the sign by rotating the point explicitly), so L_f(q) = L_f - G(1 - cos(q/2)) + 2z sin(q/2). Eq. (3) is right. The remark after it also holds: lowering the pole by delta changes the denominator at q_req by 2 delta [tan(1 - cos) + sin] = 2 delta tan(q_req/2), the same as the numerator, so C_pad rises while C_pad < 1.
- Proposition 3. Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p and ||(I - R_q)v|| = 2 sin(q/2)||v||. Eq. (6) is right. Eq. (7) follows from the triangle inequality on the two pole shifts, which differ by the depth vector of length |Delta t|, evaluated at q_req. It needs both faces to deepen by the same Delta t (S1), which is not restated.
- Corollary 1. Follows from Eq. (6) with a constant pole; the maximum over q is at q_req for q_req <= 180 deg. For equal rollers I derived the finite pole as the intersection of the q = 0 symmetry line with the common tangent at pose q: depth r tan(q/4), as stated.
- Cross-checks against the figures: bound at 90 deg, Delta h = 6 is 8.49 mm, and 4.31/8.49 = 51%; bound at 120 deg, Delta h = 2 is 3.46 mm, and 0.35/3.46 = 10%. Fig. 4(d) 2R-remote reaches about 10.4 mm = 2 sin 60 x 6 at q = 120 deg. The Study 1 pole depths (2.4-7.4 mm for faces 6-12 mm deep, at most 1.5 mm along the finger) give a pole-to-face change of about 1 mm, hence Delta_contact of roughly 2-3 mm at 9 mm, consistent with the reported 2.3 mm.

So the design section is sound. My concerns are about how tightly the studies test the propositions and about what the central quantity is.

1. Delta_contact is a position-path deviation, not a Jacobian deviation. Eq. (5) is the max distance between two paths c(q) of one face point with c0 kept. Delta_contact = 0 for all q implies the PIP column is kept, but a small Delta_contact does not bound the column error, since the column depends on the pole's derivative. And the Study 3 mechanism (a nearer face over-closes at the same setpoint) is a forward-kinematics effect at one pose. The authors half-say this ("where the faces sit"), and the post hoc rho = -0.99 with the signed normal offset supports it. "Keep the contact-surface motion" is what is shown; "keep the contact Jacobian" is a stronger title than the metric. Also only the PIP column is kept; the MCP column is explicitly not.

2. H2 is a weak test. Corollary 1 bounds fixed-axis joints. A four-bar with free pivots falling below a bound that does not apply to it is expected; the informative result is the coverage cost (0.2 points). The cross-architecture comparison is also confounded by c0: 2R-DD and RCJ "start apart", so their Delta_contact contains a q = 0 offset that Prop. 3 excludes; with it removed CFL is best in 7 of 9 cells, not 9 of 9.

3. H1 and mechanical advantage. CFL optima have worst-case MA 0.41-0.58 against 1 for the revolute joints. The coverage lead (2.4-14.6 points) is bought partly with advantage, and no equal-advantage comparison is given. The paper states the trade-off, but H1 as tested is "more coverage with MA free". Also at 90 deg 2R-remote beats the CFL, and RCJ, whose pole also descends (r tan(q/4)), is the worst architecture; Prop. 2 predicts neither, so hardware terms (x0, d, roller length) dominate there and the pole law explains the trend in thickness, not the ranking.

4. Study 3 runs only at q_req = 120 deg, where the CFL keeps the motion best (Fig. 4: about 2.3 mm at 9 mm versus 4.3 mm at 90 deg). The tolerance "about 1 mm, up to about 7 mm pads" is from this cell. Fig. 1(d) shows R recovering well under half of the true effect at 9 mm.

5. The four-bar itself is never parametrised: no link lengths, pivot positions, z(q) expression, or MA definition. A kinematics reader cannot reproduce a design. Eq. (3) is not checked numerically against the optimised designs, only Eq. (2) is.

6. Table I shows the claim is specific to a reused impedance setpoint: under torque and touch protocols the skeleton predicts as well or better (0.71, 0.88). The paper says so, and C2 is worded conditionally, which is right.

Stage 1 fit: 5 pages plus references, hypotheses with failure criteria, a concrete Stage 2 protocol with a predicted ordering (21, 72, 124%) and a named test. Evidence is simulation only with one contact model and one task, which Stage 1 allows.

Overall: correct and compact kinematics, a useful closed-form bound, and a simulation study that supports the main claim within a stated scope. The weaknesses are scope and framing, not errors. I rate it 6, Accept.
