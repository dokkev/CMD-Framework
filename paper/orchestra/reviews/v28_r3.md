ASSESSMENT NOTES:
Perspective: mechanism kinematics.

The paper makes two claims about one planar finger of a servo-driven hand. (C1) Remote actuation plus a crossed four-bar (CFL) PIP joint leaves more room for thick pads than a servo-at-joint revolute finger. (C2) When a finger is redesigned for a thicker pad, it should keep the PIP column of its contact Jacobian (the motion of the distal contact face relative to the proximal one), not its skeleton. A fixed axis cannot do this, with a closed-form lower bound, while the CFL can stay well below that bound. Simulation (Sec. IV) then tests whether Delta_contact predicts the error a reused impedance setpoint adds to measured pad effects. As asked, I focus on Section III and on whether its numbers match the study figures.

Re-derivations (all hold under the stated assumptions):
- Lemma 1. The fixed point of y -> e^{iq} y + b is P = b/(1 - e^{iq}). Since 1/(1 - e^{iq}) = (1 + i cot(q/2))/2, this gives P = (b/2)(1 + i cot(q/2)). Correct. The remark "finite pole = instantaneous centre only for a fixed axis" also checks out: differentiating c(q) = P + R_q(c0 - P) gives |p_hat - P| = 2 sin(q/2)|P'|, which vanishes on an interval only if P' = 0.
- Prop. 1. Rotating about P by q after reflecting in the vertical line through P is a reflection in the line through P tilted by q/2. So under S1–S2 the rotated distal pad is a mirror image, and the convex-set argument is right. With the pole at depth z and the deepest joint-side corner at (-G/2, t), the reflection line passes x = -(t - z) tan(q/2) at depth t. This gives G >= 2[t - z]_+ tan(q/2), which is Eq. (3). The value 2 tan 60° = 3.46 mm/mm and the ratio tan 75°/tan 45° = 3.73 ("nearly four times") are correct. The argument also survives unequal pad lengths, which the statement does not need to exclude.
- Prop. 2. The distance between the phalanx ends is twice the distance of (-G/2, -z) from the reflection line, i.e. G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2), so the denominator of Eq. (4) is right. Lowering the pole by delta adds 2 delta tan(q_req/2) to the numerator. At q_req it adds exactly the same to the denominator, because tan(1 - cos) + sin = tan. At q < q_req it adds strictly less, by sin((q - q_req)/2)/cos(q_req/2) < 0. So the claim "coverage rises until the pole reaches the face" holds for every pose in Q_eval, not only at q_req (worth saying). Below the face, G = 0 and the denominator keeps growing, so the face is the optimum.
- Rolling joint. For two equal rollers I get P = -i r tan(q/4), which confirms z = r tan(q/4).
- Prop. 3 / Eq. (7). Delta_c = (I - R_q) Delta_p, and |1 - e^{iq}| = 2 sin(q/2). Correct. Eq. (8) follows from Delta_p_Fp - Delta_p_F1 = Delta_t and the triangle inequality, provided both start points are kept.
- Cor. 1 and the "sliding" floor. Minimising max_q |(1 - e^{iq}) Delta_p + e^{iq} u| over the axis position along the face and the slide u, I get 0.586, 0.667 and 0.794 of the bound at 90°, 120° and 150°. This matches "0.59–0.80", though the last value rounds to 0.79.

Cross-checks against Fig. 3:
- 2R-remote matches the fixed-axis bound 2 sin(q_req/2) Delta_t at 90° (2.83, 5.66, 8.49 mm) and at 120° (3.46, 6.93, 10.39 mm), as claimed.
- CFL / bound ratios run from about 0.35/3.46 = 10% (120°, 5 mm) to about 4.3/8.49 = 51% (90°, 9 mm). This matches "10–51%".
- The CFL lead over 2R-DD runs from about 2.3 points (90°, 9 mm) to 14.7 points (150°, 5 mm). This matches "2.4–14.6 in 10 conditions".
- The pole depths in Sec. III-D are internally consistent (6 - 2.4 = 3.6 and 12 - 7.4 = 4.6).
- A rough check of Eq. (4) for 2R-remote at 120° (G = 2t tan 60°, t = h + 3) gives about 0.66 at 9 mm against 63.5% in Fig. 3b, which is plausible for a small x0.

The kinematic core is correct and unusually crisp for a design paper.

Problems that the scores rest on:
1. RCJ falls below the "fixed-axis bound" without explanation. Cor. 1 says the bound "also holds for a rolling joint of fixed roller radius". Yet in Fig. 3d–f the RCJ redesigns sit clearly below the dotted bound: 120°/7 mm about 3.8 vs 6.9 mm; 150°/5 mm about 1.9 vs 3.9 mm; 90°/9 mm about 7.2 vs 8.5 mm. The caption explains only 2R-DD (servo holds the face deep). Presumably the optimizer changes r. With r = t, the distance from pole to face is t(1 - tan(q/4)), which gives a rolling-joint floor of max_q 2 sin(q/2)(1 - tan(q/4)) Delta_t = 0.83 Delta_t, i.e. 0.59, 0.48 and 0.43 of the fixed-axis bound. The CFL's 10–51% is still below that, but the comparison the paper actually needs is against the best physical rolling joint, not the fixed axis. The corollary should state the variable-radius case.
2. Start offset and coverage-end reporting. Prop. 3 and Cor. 1 assume c0 is kept, but Delta_contact (Eq. 6) includes the offset at q = 0. Fig. 3g shows 2R-DD and RCJ redesigns whose faces at q = 0 are shifted by about 5 and 9 mm. The parenthetical "(7 of 9 without 2R-DD's and RCJ's start offset)" quietly admits that "least in every cell" partly depends on that offset. That offset is an artefact of reporting the coverage end of each Pareto front. A fair comparison would report Delta_contact at matched coverage, or the whole fronts.
3. Conventions and frames. Lemma 1 uses e^{iq} (counter-clockwise), while the mirror-line normal (cos q/2, -sin q/2) implies clockwise flexion. z(q) is "depth below the link line", but Fig. 2a draws z upward and Fig. 2b plots "above its axis". Three pole symbols (P, p, p_hat) and two frames (F1, Fp) are used with little separation. The tracked point c0 is "on the distal face below the fingertip", but the fingertip C is a design variable. Delta_contact equals the same value for every face point only if points are matched in a distal-face frame, which should be defined.
4. The fixed-axis impossibility depends on conventions. "A fixed axis cannot keep the contact-surface motion" rests on two conventions: the mounting rule (no spacers) and "full-width pads leave no room for lugs". A 3 mm reference finger on a spacer as deep as the 9 mm face would let a fixed axis keep c(q) exactly, at some coverage cost at 3 mm. The intro claim "Only a joint whose pole moves can" should be stated as conditional on the mounting rule.
5. Transmission and the impedance coordinate. The paper says "a joint command can undo a change of transmission". But the CFL servo turns link A'B, not q, and worst-case MA is 0.41–0.58. If K0 = 5 N m/rad is applied in servo coordinates, the PIP stiffness scales with the squared transmission ratio and is not undone by a command. If it is applied in q, the simulation idealizes away a difference that the Stage 2 servo hardware will have. The paper should say which coordinate K0 is defined in, and define MA (the "rolling joint's 0.5" is unexplained).
6. Smaller points. Fig. 2c (virtual pole about 0.85–0.9) is about 10 points above the optimized CFL (69–80%), and CFL ideal sits on CFL, so the actuator body is not what limits it; what does? Fig. 3f gives redesign Delta_contact (14, 23 mm) for 2R-remote designs that Sec. III-D calls degenerate. The −37% (Sec. IV-B, on O) and the "41% less" (abstract) truth values for the same pad swap need reconciling.

Score basis. C1 is well supported: exact clearance in the optimization, closed-form laws as estimates, and the numbers match the figures. The analytic part of C2 (Eqs. 7–9) is correct and useful. Its empirical comparison across joints, however, is weakened by items 1–2. The Stage 1 falsification plan is concrete (Page trend, predicted 21/72/124%). Writing is extremely compressed, which hurts how easily the kinematics can be checked.
