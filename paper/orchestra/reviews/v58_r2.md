ASSESSMENT NOTES:

Central claims and how well each is supported:

1. Theory: when the contact surfaces deepen by Δt, no redesign can keep both the contact-surface motion and the skeleton motion (Eq. 4), and a fixed axis cannot keep the contact-surface motion (Eq. 5). The algebra checks out. With c = R c0 + (I−R)p and |I−R| = 2 sin(q/2), Eq. (3) follows. The poles seen from F_p and F_1 differ by the frame offset Δt, which gives Eq. (4). The result is neat and easy to verify, but it is narrow:
   - It depends on the "same initial contact point" premise. Fig. 3(c) itself shows at-joint designs below the fixed-axis bound because they start from a different initial point. So "a fixed axis cannot keep that motion" is a conditional statement, and its practical force against a well-chosen fixed-axis redesign is not shown.
   - The result is a lower bound evaluated only at q_req. It says nothing about whether the CFL comes close to the bound; that part is empirical (Δ^p_d = 1.1 mm at 7 mm).
   Overall the theory is a modest but clean contribution.

2. Room and coverage: the CFL beats the at-joint finger in all 10 jointly feasible conditions, by 2.4–14.6 pp. The margin is modest. Remote actuation alone matches it at 90° and up to 5 mm at 120°, so much of the "room" benefit comes from moving the actuator, not from the articulation. The CFL's preservation advantage (Fig. 3(c)) is clearer.

3. Main empirical claim: under O's reused impedance setpoint, the error in a measured pad effect follows Δ^p_d, not Δ_skel.
   - The statistical treatment is fairly careful: permutation tests with Holm correction, partial correlations controlling Δ_skel and force deviation, two simulators and two contact models, a Skeleton control, and a self-noise floor of about 6%.
   - The Skeleton control is the strongest part of the paper. Skeleton keeps the fingertip path (Δ_skel = 0.3 mm) yet errs as much as Coverage or more.
   - Several things limit how much this evidence proves:
     (a) Everything is simulated, and no pad deforms. The "thicker, compliant pad" in Fig. 1(d) is a softer point contact or a thicker hydroelastic layer. The paper's motivation (pads that conform and spread force) is therefore not what is tested, and the headline sign reversal may depend on the contact model.
     (b) The result comes close to tautology for pad-contact tasks. If the task contacts the pads, the kinematics of the pads plausibly govern the response. The fingertip-end dial confirms the converse. So the principle reduces to "preserve the kinematics of wherever the contact occurs", which is sensible but expected, and it makes "not the skeleton" task-dependent rather than general.
     (c) The correlation set includes redesigns "held to a prescribed Δ^p_d", so the spread in Δ^p_d is engineered. This is acceptable for a controlled study, but the population of redesigns over which ρ = 0.94 holds is not representative of anything natural.
     (d) Re-referencing the setpoint cuts the error from 42% to 14%, so much of the effect is a setpoint/calibration mismatch that a practitioner would remove. The argument that a learned policy has no setpoint to re-reference rests on thin policy evidence: 3 tasks, 2 seeds, and finger walking that does not transfer to Drake even on O.
     (e) Table II correlations for the pad-turned dial (0.53) and box pivot (0.43) over 18 redesigns are weak, with no significance reported.
     (f) "Pre-registered" is asserted but not verifiable (no registry or anonymized link).
     (g) Basic counts are missing: placements per hand, PPO evaluation episodes, and optimization seeds and budgets.

4. Hardware: the title promises the design of a robot hand, but the CoDe Hand appears only as CAD and USD renders. There is no physical prototype, no measured pad effect, and no real-world grasp. For RSS this is a significant gap, because the claim concerns how real compliant interfaces are evaluated.

5. Choosing CoDe from the Pareto front of Eq. (9) ("keeps both best at near-best coverage") is a subjective pick, and its sensitivity is not shown. The ablation (17% → 10% with Δ^1_d) helps somewhat.

My scores rest on these points. The paper has a clear, well-posed and original framing (what to preserve when the interface changes), a correct small piece of kinematics, and a well-controlled simulation study with a good negative control. However, there is no hardware validation and no deformable pad model, the result is partly expected for pad-contact tasks, and the policy evidence is thin. Presentation is very dense and symbol-heavy for a short paper. I lean toward rejection, but I could be persuaded.

PRESENTATION AND CLARITY ITEMS:

1. The abstract is very dense and stacks several qualified claims ("from the same initial contact point", "wherever both are feasible", "with the contact-force difference controlled"). Lead with the one main finding and the Skeleton control. The final sentence ("A redesign for coverage alone reports that...") is hard to parse on a first read.
2. There are too many near-identical symbols for a 5-page paper (Δ^p_d, Δ^1_d, Δ_skel, Δp, Δp_1, Δt, t, h_i). Δ^1_d is introduced only in Eq. (7) yet is essential to CoDe's definition and the 17%→10% ablation. Consider a small notation table or a figure that marks each deviation.
3. In Sec. III-A the "Pole" subsection is hard to find. The section opens with a summary paragraph, and Eq. (1)'s complex-number form appears without saying that y is a point of the distal phalanx in the proximal frame.
4. Eq. (2): define "joint-side corners" and explain why the pole sits "midway". The 3.46 mm/mm figure (2 tan 60°) should be derived in one clause.
5. The sentence "Eq. (3) holds for the fingertip too, with the pole seen from F_1" should state explicitly that the same-initial-point premise holds for the fingertip because L_f is fixed.
6. Fig. 1(c): the caption says Coverage "keeps neither fully", but its skeleton curve sits below CoDe's. Clarify that CoDe deliberately gives up the skeleton while Coverage is mediocre on both.
7. Fig. 1(d): "thicker, compliant pad" on O is modeled only as a stiffness change on O's own geometry (Sec. IV-A). State this in the caption to avoid overclaiming.
8. Sec. III-C: "∆^p_d = 0 means the same contact points with the same contact Jacobian at every flexion; ∆^p_d measures the departure from both" deserves one more sentence of justification, since a zero path deviation implies equal derivatives only if it holds identically in q.
9. Sec. III-D cites "Sec. III-D2" for CoDe's definition, but CoDe is defined in Sec. III-C (Eq. 9). Fix the cross-reference.
10. Table I: the columns "convex", "touch", "force" and "setpoint" are not defined in the text. "compl. pad" and "stiffness" need distinguishing. Bold entries are not explained.
11. Table II: give the number of placements and indicate significance for the ρ values. The bottom rows (Δ^p_d, Δ_skel) list only three hands, which is confusing under columns labeled by contact model.
12. Fig. 4: the circle and diamond markers and the thin quartile bands are hard to read at print size. "Load deviation" for the hold task should say which load (pull-out?).
13. Fig. 5: "palm shift" appears only under the bars and is defined only in the text. Put the definition in the caption. Units of "share of O's performance kept" per task (success rate? angle turned?) are not stated.
14. Results paragraph 1: "The objective, not the freedom, does this" is cryptic. Say "the choice of objective, not the extra pivot-height freedom, reduces the error".
15. Explain "Holm-corrected" (over which family of tests?) and "permutation p < 0.001" (which statistic is permuted?) briefly, or move both to a footnote.
16. Introduction: "the joint axes and the fingertip path that hand design has always preserved" is an overclaim without citation. Soften it or cite.
17. Fig. 2(a): the shaded CFL region and its upper boundary at about 30 mm are not explained, and "contact faces, 3–9 mm pads" overlaps the RCJ wedge. A one-line legend would help.
18. Fig. 3(a): the scatter's markers for infeasible designs (×) at 150° overlap. Its y-axis label "C̄_pad (%)" is small.
19. Several references lack page numbers or venues consistently ([4] and [5] are arXiv; [5] lacks "preprint"). [16] is cited for both the RCJ and the equal-roller form; confirm it is the right source for the pole bound r tan(q/4).
20. The terms "CoDe" (method/hand) and "CFL" (mechanism) are used close together, and "CFL (ours)" in figures vs. "CoDe Hand" in text may confuse readers. One sentence early on stating the mapping would help.
