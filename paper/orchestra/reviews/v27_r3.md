ASSESSMENT NOTES:
Perspective: mechanism kinematics.

I re-derived every result in Section III and checked the reported numbers against Figs. 2-5 and Table I.

What checks out:
- Lemma 1. P = b/(1-e^{iq}). Since 1/(1-e^{iq}) = (1 + i cot(q/2))/2, the closed form is correct.
- Proposition 1. The mirror line through the pole has normal (cos(q/2), -sin(q/2)). The deepest joint-side face corner (-G/2, -(t-z)) stays on the proximal side iff G >= 2(t-z) tan(q/2), so Eq. (3) with the [.]_+ clip is correct. The convex-set lemma also holds: a convex set that crosses the line contains a point of the line, and that point is fixed by the reflection. One point to note: S2 really requires the finite pole's x to sit at the gap midpoint for every q, because the composed reflections only reproduce the posed distal pad then. So "exact when the pole moves in depth alone" is the true scope of the "iff".
- Proposition 2. The phalanx-end chord is twice the distance to the mirror line, G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2). Eq. (4) follows.
- The "lower the pole by delta" argument is exact, which is a nice result. At q_req the denominator grows by 2δ[tan(Q/2)(1-cos(Q/2)) + sin(Q/2)] = 2δ tan(Q/2), the same as the numerator. At q < q_req it grows less (the difference is cos(q/2)(tan(Q/2) - tan(q/2)) >= 0). So the C̄_pad average also rises.
- Rolling joint. For two equal rollers, the finite pole is (r, -r tan(q/4)) in the frame of the line of centres, so z = r tan(q/4) is correct and the pole moves in depth only.
- 2 tan 60° = 3.46 mm per mm, and the 150°/90° ratio is 3.73 ("nearly four times").
- Proposition 3. Δc(q) = (I - R_q)Δp, and |1 - e^{iq}| = 2 sin(q/2), so Eq. (7) is exact. The frames F_p and the link line differ by the face depth t, so Δp_face - Δp_skel = Δt·n. The triangle inequality then gives Eq. (8).
- Corollary 1 equality case. Fig. 4 reproduces the bound line for 2R-remote: 2.83/5.66/8.49 mm at 90°, 3.46/6.93/10.39 mm at 120°, and 3.86 to 11.59 mm at 150°.
- CFL ratios read from Fig. 4:
  - 90°: 0.44 / 0.33 / 0.51
  - 120°: 0.10 / 0.12 / 0.23
  - 150°: 0.21 / 0.22 / 0.31
  These match "0.35-4.31 mm, 10-51%".
- The "fixed axis free to slide" figure. I minimised max_q |e^{iq} a + (1 - e^{iq})(b + i)| over the contact-point slide a and the axis slide b, which reproduces it: 0.590, 0.670 and 0.796 of the bound at 90°, 120° and 150°. The last value rounds to 0.80, not 0.79.
- Fig. 2c is consistent with Eq. (4) for a pole on the face: (L_f - x0)/(L_f + 2t sin(q/2)).
- With t = h + 3 mm (implied by "face deepens from 6 to 12 mm") and x0 ≈ 2 mm, Eq. (4) reproduces 2R-remote's Fig. 3 coverage within about one point at 120° (about 0.82 at 3 mm, about 0.63 at 9 mm).
- The CFL-vs-2R-DD margins (2.4-14.6 points, 10 conditions) and the 120° crossover between 5 and 7 mm read correctly off Fig. 3.
- The R design's Δcontact (0.35, 0.85, 2.35 mm at 120°) is consistent across Fig. 4b, Fig. 5a and the Stage-2 protocol.

Where the kinematic argument is weaker than the text claims:

(1) The "no fixed axis can" statement (Intro, Corollary 1, "Why the CFL") is really a statement about the axis-to-face distance.
- It holds only under the mounting rule, together with z ≡ 0 or "the pin sits above the mounting surface". The proof's |Δp| >= |Δt| is true by definition once the axis cannot go deeper.
- "Wherever the new axis is placed along the face" silently excludes placing it deeper. "Full-width pads leave no room for lugs" is a design rule, not kinematics.
- The corollary should say this assumption outright. Spacers are already treated as the exception; a deeper axis with lateral lugs or a deeper phalanx is the other exception.

(2) What is kept is not the finger's contact Jacobian.
- It is the PIP column of the distal face relative to the proximal phalanx, with the MCP held at 0.
- A thicker proximal pad moves the proximal face away from the MCP axis by Δt. So the MCP column of every contact on that face changes by Δt per radian whatever the PIP linkage does.
- The title and abstract claim more than Section III shows. The polycentric MCP is listed as future work, but its necessity follows directly from Eq. (7) applied at the MCP and should be stated.

(3) Δcontact as computed does not match the premise of Prop. 3 and Cor. 1 ("same c0").
- Fig. 4d shows nonzero ‖Δc(0)‖ for 2R-DD (≈5.3 mm) and RCJ (≈9.3 mm). For RCJ at 120°, 9 mm, this q = 0 offset is the whole reported Δcontact.
- The "point on the distal face below the fingertip" is apparently not invariant across redesigns. As a result, Fig. 4 mixes contact-point placement with contact-surface motion.
- The paper concedes that without the offset the CFL is least in 7 of 9 cells, not "every cell" as Sec. III-E states.

(4) The CFL optima lie outside the hypotheses of Props. 1-2.
- The pole moves up to 1.5 mm along the finger, which violates S2.
- At 120° the pole sits 2.4-7.4 mm deep under a 6-12 mm face, i.e. 3.6-4.6 mm above the face. Prop. 2 says the best joint turns about a point on the face. Why the optimiser stops about 4 mm short (transmission angle? closure? servo collision?) is not explained, yet it is the binding mechanism fact behind Contribution 1.
- With those pole depths and the x0 that fits 2R-remote, Eq. (4) at q_req predicts about 0.86 (3 mm) and 0.76 (9 mm) for the CFL against about 0.78 and 0.70 in Fig. 3. The gap may be set at an intermediate flexion, or x0 may differ per joint, but the paper gives neither x0 nor G nor z(q) on Q_eval per design. The only accuracy figure is a median 0.05 mm (no maximum) for Eq. (3).
- Also at 90° the remote fixed axis beats the CFL at every thickness. The CFL's room advantage appears only for ≥120° and thick pads, which the abstract's "every pad thickness and required flexion" framing (against 2R-DD) obscures.

(5) Smaller points.
- The Stage-2 trio sits essentially on the Eq. (8) bound: M has 2.3 + 4.6 = 6.9 against 2 sin 60°·4 = 6.93. This is fine within rounding, but worth reporting to two decimals as a sanity check.
- Δcontact is a max over [0, q_req], while the experiment acts near q0 and q0 + 5°. It is unclear why the global max, rather than Δc at the operating pose, is the predictor.
- Stage-2 realisability: R, M and U differ in pivot locations A, A', B, B'. How do "swapping CFL links" realise them on one finger, and does four-pin backlash stay well below the 0.9 mm that separates R from O?
- Fig. 4c plots 2R-remote redesigns at 150° for 7 and 9 mm, which Fig. 3 calls degenerate.
- Notation and figures:
  - Fig. 2b's axis says "above its axis", while the text uses depth below the link line.
  - The sign of R_{π/2} depends on flexion orientation.
  - The relation of L_vp to G is never stated.
  - Fig. 2c's h axis (2-8 mm) differs from the studied 3-9 mm.

Scores.
- The analysis is correct and compact. The closed forms are genuinely useful design laws, and Eq. (7) and the fixed-axis bound give the redesign problem a clean kinematic footing.
- Most numbers I could check are consistent.
- The weaknesses are scope and definition issues: a mounting-rule-conditional corollary, a PIP-only "contact Jacobian", and a Δcontact that includes a c0 offset. A careful revision can fix them; they do not invalidate the results.
- Soundness good, Presentation good but dense, Contribution good. Accept for Stage 1.
