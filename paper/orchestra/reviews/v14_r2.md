THOUGHT:

My lens is contact mechanics, tactile sensing and how interface experiments are run and confounded. The paper raises a confound I agree is real and seldom named. When a hand is rebuilt to carry a thicker pad or tactile module, the hand changes too, and a measured "interface effect" mixes both. The paper makes two claims. C1: a remote-servo, crossed four-bar (CFL) PIP leaves more room for pads. C2: a redesign should keep the "contact Jacobian", i.e. the motion of the distal contact face relative to the proximal face, not the skeleton. The pole-based analysis (Lemma 1, Props. 1-3, Eq. 7, Corollary 1) is compact and, as far as I can check, correct. Prop. 3 follows directly from c(M,q) - c(M0,q) = (I - R_q)(p - p0). Eq. 7 neatly formalises why a thicker pad at fixed mount depth cannot keep both the skeleton and the face motion. The numerical checks against the evaluator (1e-13, 0.05 mm) are reassuring. Study 1 and Study 2 are kinematic optimisation studies and I have few concerns about them beyond optimiser fairness.

The weight of the paper, and of my review, falls on Study 3 (H3). That is where the paper claims that the error in a measured interface effect "follows" Delta_contact and "not" the other candidates. My main concerns:

1. What is being estimated. E(H) = |Delta(H) - Delta(O)| / |Delta(O)| measures a hand x interface interaction: does a friction/stiffness/shape change have the same effect on hand H as on O? The confound described in the introduction is mostly a main effect: comparing a thick-pad hand with a thin-pad hand mixes the interface with the hand. A redesign could preserve the friction effect and still shift the baseline limit load a lot. The paper should state its estimand explicitly and report the baseline shift L(H) - L(O) at fixed pad parameters as well. There is also a conceptual point. R removes the geometric consequence of thickness by construction, so in the R design "the interface" means its material and surface properties, not its thickness. That is a defensible choice, but it should be stated.

2. Near-tautology under the reused position command. With Coulomb friction, the slip load under a friction change scales with normal force, so E_friction is to first order |N_H/N_O - 1|. Under a reused position command with a stiff joint and soft contact, N is set by over-closure, i.e. by where the face meets the object, which is exactly what Delta_contact encodes. The stiffness effect, a change in contact stiffness at fixed over-closure, is force-mediated in the same way. So rho = 0.83/0.80 is close to built in. The authors partly saw this. The torque protocol (rho = 0.75) and the post hoc partial correlation given force (0.80) are the results that actually support "geometry itself matters". They should be the headline, and they should be pre-specified for Stage 2, not post hoc.

3. Correlational inference over a constructed design set. The 15/14/16/45 "hands" are not a sample from any population. They were built to span Delta_contact and to decorrelate it from Delta_skel ("keep or change the skeleton as much as possible"). Spearman rho over such a set depends on the design of experiments. The negative rho for Delta_skel (-0.40, -0.51) is a symptom of that sampling, not a property of hands. Each candidate gets a one-sided test against zero, but H3 is a comparative claim ("follows Delta_contact, not ..."), and nothing compares correlations directly (e.g. a bootstrap CI on rho_contact - rho_other, or a joint rank regression). In the torque column, coverage (-0.52) and thickness (0.50) on 16 hands are not shown to differ from 0.75. The abstract's "not in skeleton, coverage, thickness or transmission" overreaches: non-significance is read as absence of relation. The pad-shape result rests on six hands, which gives a CI on rho that is nearly uninformative. One more issue: does Table I include the contrast (fixed-axis, skeleton-kept) family? It is reported separately with errors of 95-228%. If it is in the table it would carry high leverage.

4. Selection on outcome. E is computed only over hands that "hold the cylinder" and only over the placements where both H and O hold. Hands, or placements within hands, that fail are dropped. The failures most plausibly sit at high Delta_contact, and different hands are evaluated on different placement subsets. The authors should report how many hands and placements were dropped per setting and run a sensitivity analysis where a failure counts as maximal error.

5. Contact model versus the quantity of interest. All pads share one contact model, and faces are rigid surfaces with a MuJoCo-Warp soft contact. The R designs keep Delta_contact at roughly 0.35-2 mm. Real compliant pads at grasp loads indent by about the same amount, and indentation scales with thickness (k ~ EA/h). For a deforming thick pad, the "contact surface motion" is not a rigid-face property, and the contact Jacobian moves with load. The robustness sweeps (contact stiffness x10, impratio x0.3/x3, substeps, joint stiffness x100) are good practice. But they vary solver parameters, not the physics that is missing. The slip criterion also matters: MuJoCo regularised friction creeps under tangential load. The paper does not specify the friction cone, the slip threshold or the rationale for the 10 mm/s pull rate, and each of these sets the measured "limit load".

6. The Stage 2 hardware test does not test H3 as stated. Only O, R and U are built, and the decisive test is R vs U on 7 mm pads. U differs from R in skeleton, coverage, transmission and Delta_contact together, so "R errs less than U" cannot attribute the error to Delta_contact rather than to any of those. Two hands cannot give a correlation. There is also a problem with the reference: real 3 and 7 mm pads differ in compliance, so O's effect Delta(O) is itself confounded. With the absolute value in E, that offset does not cancel when E_R is compared with E_U. The authors should define the hardware error explicitly (e.g. signed difference, or the effect on R vs U directly). They should also add at least one hand that matches U's Delta_contact while matching R's skeleton, or vice versa. Without it, the hardware result cannot separate the contact-surface hypothesis from the generic "U is a worse redesign". The plan also needs a power or minimum-detectable-effect statement for 20 paired units with 5 repeats each.

7. Study 1 fairness. CFL alone gets a neighbour refinement. The authors say the result also holds without it, which is good. But seed-to-seed spread across the three GA seeds is not reported for any architecture. Gaps of 2.4 points are within what I would expect from optimiser variance on a constrained mixed design space. H1 is only as strong as the search on the 2R-DD side.

8. Terminology and clarity. "Contact Jacobian" in the Montana / Murray-Li-Sastry sense maps joint velocities to contact-frame velocities. It depends on the object, the contact frame and the normal. Here it is the PIP column of a point on the distal face in the proximal frame, i.e. the relative rigid motion of the two faces. Calling this the contact Jacobian is a stretch, and "keep the relative face motion" would be more accurate. The paper is very dense. CFL is never expanded. S1/S2, x0, G and T_i are introduced in a single pass. Sentences like "The flexion command passes through each hand's own transmission table, which undoes the transmission change" need unpacking. The abstract is crowded with numbers. Fig. 3 and Fig. 5 are hard to read at print size.

Positives: the hypotheses state their own failure conditions. Thresholds were fixed before the runs. Holm correction is applied. Intervals resample hands. Post hoc analyses are labelled as post hoc. The measurement resolution (one load step = 2.5-5.6% of O's effect) is reported, and a force-matched control was run. This is more methodological care than most hand-design papers show. A CAD hand exists and the Stage 2 plan is concrete. For a Stage 1 abstract, hypotheses and methods are essentially complete. My concerns are about whether the evidence isolates the claimed mechanism and whether the hardware test can falsify it. All of them are addressable before Stage 2. I lean to accept, conditional on the authors (a) promoting the force-controlled analysis, (b) adding direct comparisons between correlations and outcome-selection sensitivity, and (c) adding a hardware hand that decorrelates Delta_contact from the other candidates.
