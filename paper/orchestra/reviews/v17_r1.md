THOUGHT:
I read this as a hand designer who has built servo-chain hands and run pad/tactile comparisons on them. The paper makes two claims about one planar PIP joint: (C1) remote actuation plus a crossed four-bar (CFL) leaves more pad length than a servo-at-joint finger, and (C2) when a finger is redesigned for a thicker pad, what should be held constant is the path of the distal contact face in the proximal-face frame, not the skeleton, because the change in that path (Delta_contact) is what biases a measured interface effect.

What I find convincing. The pole argument in Sec. III is short, correct as far as I can check, and useful. Prop. 3 is one line (Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, norm 2 sin(q/2)|Delta p|), and Eq. (7) and Corollary 1 follow from it. The clearance rule 2 tan(q/2) per mm of face depth (Eq. 2, Fig. 2) is exactly the rule of thumb a designer needs and explains why proximal pads on servo chains stay thin. The ablation 2R-DD / 2R-remote / RCJ / CFL separates remote actuation from the polycentric joint, which is the right decomposition. Each hypothesis states how it fails, post hoc analyses are flagged, and the Stage 2 protocol names the hands, the predicted errors (21, 72, 124%) and the test. For a Stage-1 abstract, the problem statement, hypotheses and methods are complete; this is what the venue asks for.

What I doubt. (1) The C2 evidence rests on a protocol in which one open-loop flexion command (or torque) is replayed on a finger whose contact face has moved by millimetres. That the grasp force then differs, and with it the limit load, is close to tautological: the paper itself reports that the route is over-closure (force deviation tracks the error at rho = 0.94), that the fixed-axis family presses 2.2-4.2x harder, and that matching contact force leaves 3-7%. So the practical reading may be "normalise the grasp force" rather than "redesign the joint". The equal-torque result (rho = 0.75, partial 0.80 with a wide interval, post hoc) is the only evidence that geometry matters beyond preload, and it is thin. (2) Everything is simulation with pads as a contact model (a stiffness in kN/m, friction coefficient, a 2 mm crown) in a rigid-body engine. A 7-9 mm soft pad shears, bulges and changes its patch; those effects are exactly what an interface study measures and may be as large as the geometric bias. (3) Samples are small and constructed: 14-16 hands per setting, chosen "at set Delta_contact", with intervals such as [0.33, 0.99] and [0.34, 0.98]; rho = 0.94 for shape is on six hands. The error E is normalised by |Delta(O)|, which inflates when the true effect is small. (4) Study 4, which carries the headline "48% more vs 41% less", is a post hoc reading of Study 3 runs, with a counterfactual that cannot exist on hardware. (5) C1 is measured only by the authors' own coverage metric on their own parameterisations of the baselines. Nothing links coverage points to grasp outcome, and the costs of the CFL (mechanical advantage 0.41-0.58 unconstrained, pose-dependent transmission, lateral/torsional stiffness, pivot clearance and backlash, link strength at this scale, servo mass on the proximal phalanx) are not weighed. Backlash of a small crossed four-bar could itself be of the order of the 0.35-4.3 mm Delta_contact being claimed. (6) Naming: "contact Jacobian" is defined in Eq. (4) as a derivative, but the quantity that is kept and tested is a positional max deviation of a path, including a start offset. Title says "CoDe Hand", but the hand is CAD and the analysis is one planar joint with MCP held; the paper admits the MCP column is not kept. (7) Density: Sec. V packs dozens of numbers per paragraph; the setup of Study 3 (how hands were chosen, what "placements" are, which hands are dropped because they do not hold) took me several readings.

Desired outcome: I lean accept for Stage 1 because the hypotheses and methods are complete, falsifiable and the kinematic core is sound and new to me in this framing. But the authors should confront the "this is just preload" objection head-on, state the CFL's mechanical costs, and make the Stage 2 hardware test able to distinguish geometry from force. Score 6, with soundness "fair to good" and clarity medium.
