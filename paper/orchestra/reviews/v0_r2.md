THOUGHT:

The paper argues that experiments comparing contact interfaces (thick pads, tactile modules) on robot fingers are confounded by the finger redesign that a new interface forces. It proposes using the "contact-centric kinematics" (the motion of the distal contact face seen from the proximal face) as the quantity to hold fixed, analyses joint–pad conflicts through the finite pole of the distal displacement, and runs planar optimisation studies (4 architectures x 4 thicknesses x 3 flexion requirements). For an RSS Stage-1 extended abstract the question is whether the problem, hypotheses and methodology are fully specified and whether the insights hold up even under negative results. Preliminary experiments are acceptable.

Intuitions, positive:
- The confound framing (interface effect vs. embodiment change) is real and under-discussed. Saying explicitly that "kinematics invariance is not contact-mechanics invariance" is a good call.
- The finite-pole view gives a single lens over revolute, rolling-contact and four-bar joints. The fixed-axis slopes 2 tan(q/2) = 2.00/3.46/7.46 check out, and so does the Fig. 2 example (5 tan 60° = 8.66). Eq. (4), |Δc| = 2 sin(q/2)|Δp|, follows directly from c = p + R(c0 − p). The fixed-axis bound is tight for 2R-remote (Table I matches the bound exactly at 90° and 120°), which is a nice internal consistency check.
- The paper is honest about its losses: the revolute finger leads at 90°, H2 holds only partially at 9 mm, and "none found" is labelled as a statement about the search. Every hypothesis has a stated "if false" branch, which is exactly what the Stage-1 format asks for.
- The numerical checks of the analysis (pole-based coverage vs. evaluator within 1e-13, the four-axis test, first-contact prediction) are good practice.

Intuitions, concerns (most important first):
1. The H2 claim "only a joint that moves its pole can keep the contact-centric kinematics" is undercut by the paper's own corollary. Eq. (5) depends on |Δh − Δd|, so a fixed-axis finger that recesses the thicker pad (Δd = Δh) has zero bound. The paper itself attributes 2R-DD and RCJ falling below the bound to "mount depth shrinks". The limit on Δd (the structure S must support the pad, minimum phalanx wall or link thickness, the location of servo and bearing bodies) is never stated. Without it, H2 is really about packaging depth, not pole motion. Also, Table I and Fig. 4 plot the bound with Δd = 0, which overstates CFL's relative advantage (10–51% "of the bound") whenever Δd > 0 was available to the others.
2. The invariant itself goes unjustified as the right one, and redesign moves the confound rather than removing it. CFL "absorbs the interface by changing the skeleton's motion", i.e. the link lengths change. That changes the actuator-to-joint transmission (MA profile, torque at the contact), which the paper lists among the embodiment confounds (actuation, force transmission). MA is "reported, not constrained" but no redesigned MA profiles are shown. There is also no argument or evidence for how large a Dc (0.35–4.3 mm, up to ~5% of Lf) is acceptable for an interface study. Without such a tolerance, "partial preservation" cannot be interpreted. The fallback recommendation ("report Dc as a covariate") is reasonable but weak.
3. The optimisation evidence is noisy relative to the effects claimed. CFL runs spread 1.3–3.4 points across seeds, and continuation raised cells by up to 2.1 points. Yet 2R-remote's 90° leads are 1.9–4.1 points, and the 120° crossover (6 mm, 0.740 vs 0.729) is a 1.1-point difference. With 3 seeds (Study 1) and 2 seeds (Study 2), the crossover and lead claims do not exceed search variance. The Study 2 numbers are the "coverage end" of a Pareto front, so Dc is not even being minimised at that point. Reporting the minimum-Dc end, or Dc at a coverage floor, would be the cleaner test of H2. The 90°/7 mm CFL-ideal jump (5.70 vs ≤0.65 mm) shows that the coverage-end selection is unstable.
4. The coverage metric is ad hoc and dominates H1: an inscribed cylinder, three evaluation poses (which collapse to a single pose at qreq = 90°), occlusion ignored, links and belts on other layers unchecked. H1 then becomes "which joint maximises this specific metric". Its link to the paper's main question (comparability) is indirect. H1 reads more like a packaging study than a test of the confound thesis.
5. Methodological fairness: the 2R-remote actuator pose is fixed (10 mm proximal, 15 mm dorsal, 15°) rather than optimised. The 2R-DD and RCJ "none found" cells at 150° may reflect that choice or the search. The CFL search received extra effort (re-seeding from neighbours and from Study 2), which biases best-found comparisons in CFL's favour.
6. The H3 protocol lacks success criteria. There is no numeric tolerance on the reached flexion or on the measured vs. predicted Dc, no specified tracking method or accuracy, no trial counts or statistics for the grasp protocol, and no named interfaces. "Count as interface effects only with Dc reported" needs to say how Dc will enter the analysis (e.g. as a covariate in which model?). A paper titled "CoDe Hand" has no hand yet. That is acceptable for Stage 1, but the protocol must then be fully pre-specified.
7. Theory novelty is modest. Lemma 1 is the classical finite-displacement pole (Bottema–Roth), and Prop. 1 is the separating axis theorem. The novelty lies in the application (Props. 2–4, Cor. 1). Several hypotheses (S1: equal face depths, S2: corners equidistant from the pole) are restrictive, and it is unclear how many optima satisfy them. Lemma 1's sign: (I − R)^{-1} = ½(I + cot(q/2) J) for J = +90°, so the extracted "½ b − cot(q/2) Jb" needs a sign convention for J. Prop. 3's denominator should be derived or at least explained.
8. Related work misses literature that is directly relevant: interchangeable or modular fingertips and grippers, existing comparative fingertip and tactile-sensor studies done on a single hand, and polycentric (four-bar) joint design via instant-centre placement (e.g. prosthetic knees and fingers). The latter is the closest prior art to "placing the pole".
9. Clarity: the notation is dense and overloaded (C is both the interface and the pads, A appears in both H and M, and H = (E,Q,A) is never used again). Contribution (2) has a duplicated "clearance". Fig. 1 prints (ℓ1+ℓ2)/Lf (77/52/52/54%) while the caption gives C̄pad (0.739/0.593/0.593/0.560), which confuses the reader about which figure is the objective. Fig. 4(d) shows nonzero distance at q = 0 for RCJ and 2R-DD (the reference contact point c0 changed), which should be explained relative to Prop. 4.

Overall: an interesting, careful and honest formulation with sound elementary geometry and a clear hypothesis structure. However, the central H2 claim ignores the mount-depth escape that its own bound exposes, the chosen invariant leaves transmission/MA confounds untouched, and key quantitative comparisons sit within search noise. For Stage 1, where methodology must be fully fleshed out, these are fixable but material issues. I lean toward a borderline score of 5 and Reject. I would raise it if the authors bound Δd structurally, report MA changes under redesign, define a Dc tolerance, and pre-specify the H3 criteria.
