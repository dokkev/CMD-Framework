ASSESSMENT NOTES:
Perspective: mechanism kinematics.

Central claims. (C1) With remote actuation and a crossed four-bar (CFL) PIP joint, a servo-driven finger keeps more pad coverage than a servo-at-joint revolute (2R-DD) at every pad thickness and required flexion. (C2) A redesign for a thicker pad should keep the contact-surface motion c(q), which the paper calls the PIP column of the contact Jacobian. A fixed axis cannot do this: Eq. (9) gives a closed-form lower bound on the change. The CFL can be redesigned at 10-51% of that bound while losing at most 0.2 points of coverage. (C3) In simulation, under a reused impedance setpoint, the error a redesign adds to a measured pad effect follows Delta_contact rather than Delta_skel. A Stage-2 hardware test with a Page trend test is specified to refute it.

Re-derivation of Section III. I re-derived every result in III-B. All of them hold under their stated assumptions.
- Lemma 1. P = e^{iq}P + b gives P = b/(1 - e^{iq}). Since 1/(1 - e^{iq}) = 1/2 + (i/2)cot(q/2), P = (b/2)(1 + i cot(q/2)). Correct.
- Prop. 1. Mirroring in the vertical line through P and then rotating by q about P is a reflection in that line turned by q/2, with normal (cos q/2, -sin q/2) for clockwise flexion. The argument that a convex set and its mirror image are disjoint iff the set stays on one side of the line is right. The deepest joint-side corner of the face, at (-G/2, -(t - z)) from P, is the binding point. The condition is G cos(q/2) >= 2(t - z) sin(q/2), which is Eq. (3) including the [.]_+. For the fixed axis, 2 tan 60 deg = 3.46 mm per mm. Correct.
- Prop. 2. The phalanx ends, on the link line at height z above P, lie G cos(q/2) + 2z sin(q/2) apart. Substituting into L_f(q) gives the denominator of Eq. (4). I also checked the claim that lowering the pole by delta adds the same amount to the numerator and the denominator. The numerator grows by 2 delta tan(Q/2). The denominator grows by 2 delta [tan(Q/2)(1 - cos(Q/2)) + sin(Q/2)] = 2 delta tan(Q/2). The claim is exact at q_req. At q < q_req the denominator grows by less, because tan(q/2) < tan(Q/2), so the monotonicity argument holds for the whole C̄_pad average. Below the face ([t - z]_+ = 0) only the denominator grows, so the optimum is at z = t. Correct.
- Prop. 3 and Eq. (7). The relation c = P + R_q(c0 - P) gives Delta c = (I - R_q) Delta p, and |1 - e^{iq}| = 2 sin(q/2). Given c0 and phi = q, keeping c(q) is equivalent to keeping P(q), since P = (I - R_q)^{-1}(c - R_q c0). It is also equivalent to keeping the instantaneous centre, since p̂ = c - R_{-pi/2} c'. So the statement that keeping c(q) keeps the column, and the statement that a joint command cannot undo a change of c(q), are both kinematically sound.
- Eq. (8). The pole shifts measured from the face and from the link line differ by Delta t along the depth direction. The triangle inequality at q_req then gives the bound. This requires both start points to be kept.
- Rollers. For two equal rollers, b = 2r(e^{iq/2} - e^{iq}), which gives P = r(1 + i tan(q/4)). The stated z = r tan(q/4) is correct.
- Sliding contact point. "A fixed axis whose contact point may slide stays above 0.59-0.80 of the bound." I minimised max_q ||(I - R_q)(a, Delta t) + s R_q e_x|| over the axis position a and the start shift s. The result is 0.586, 0.667 and 0.794 of the bound at 90, 120 and 150 deg. Confirmed.

Cross-check of numbers against Fig. 3.
- Fixed-axis bound. For Delta t = 6 mm, the bound is 8.49, 10.39 and 11.59 mm at 90/120/150 deg. 2R-remote reads 8.5 and 10.4 at 90 and 120 deg, and 3.8 against 3.86 at 150 deg and 5 mm. So "meets the bound exactly" checks out.
- CFL Delta_contact. Read from Fig. 3d-f: 1.25/1.85/4.31, 0.35/0.9/2.35 and 0.75/1.75/3.65 mm. Against the bound these are 0.10-0.51, consistent with "10-51%". The extremes are 0.35/3.46 at 120 deg and 5 mm, and 4.31/8.49 at 90 deg and 9 mm.
- CFL minus 2R-DD coverage. The gaps read about 2.5 to 14.6 points over the 10 cells. The largest is at 150 deg and 5 mm. Consistent.
- Fig. 3g and Fig. 3e agree: CFL max about 2.3 near 60 deg, 2R-DD 5.8, RCJ 9.3 at q = 0.
- Pole depths in III-D. 6 - 2.4 = 3.6 and 12 - 7.4 = 4.6, consistent. I plugged these depths into Eq. (4) with x0 of about 9 mm. That reproduces the CFL's 78% to 69% at 120 deg, so the closed-form law and the optimizer agree well. The paper should state x0 and show this comparison itself.
- Preload. 0.75x and 3.3x give 25% and 230%. Consistent.

Main concerns.
(1) Delta_contact is a path distance, not a Jacobian distance. Eq. (6) takes the maximum over q of ||Delta c||, including q = 0. The Jacobian column [c'; 1] is unchanged by a constant offset of c. Fig. 3g shows that 2R-DD's (about 5.3 mm) and RCJ's (about 9.3 mm) Delta_contact are dominated by, or for RCJ entirely due to, a start offset. "Least in every cell" becomes "7 of 9" without these offsets. In Sec. IV the error mechanism is preload, a zeroth-order effect of where the face sits at the setpoint. The evidence therefore supports "keep the contact-surface path". It does not yet support "keep the contact Jacobian" as the title states. Report the offset and max ||Delta c'|| separately.
(2) The corollary's preconditions are not enforced where the bound is used. Corollary 1 and Eq. (8) assume four things: c0 is kept; Delta t = Delta h; the axis lies on the link line or stays above the mounting surface; and the roller radius is fixed. Eq. (11) does not enforce c0. 2R-DD violates Delta t = Delta h, and RCJ enlarges its rollers. Yet Fig. 3d-f draws one bound with Delta t = Delta h for all joints. The rule that a pin cannot sit below the mounting surface ("full-width pads leave no room for lugs") is a packaging rule, not a kinematic fact. The introduction's claim that "only a joint whose pole moves can" keep the motion rests on it. A joint-specific bound using each design's actual Delta t would be cleaner.
(3) The exact laws hold only under S1-S2, and the optimizer is heuristic. The CFL violates S1-S2: z(q) varies and the pole drifts by up to 1.5 mm. The optima come from a GA or NSGA-II run with three seeds. Only the CFL gets the extra refinement from neighbouring designs, and no optimality gap is reported. The 2R-DD space is small, so C1 is probably safe. Still, C1's "at every pad thickness and flexion" rests on unequal search effort.
(4) Transmission modelling. The joint stiffness is mapped as K0/(dtheta/dq)^2. This drops the (d^2 theta/dq^2) tau term, which is not small for a four-bar under the very preload the paper identifies as the mechanism. The paper also does not say at which q dtheta/dq is evaluated. Mechanical advantage is reported for the coverage optima (0.41-0.58) but not for the R redesigns. The comparison to "a rolling joint's 0.5" is undefined.
(5) Presentation. Several quantities are not defined: x0, r_{2,s}, r_{1,e}, and the depth reference of Fig. 2b ("above its axis"). Fig. 3f plots Delta_contact of 14 and 23 mm for degenerate 2R-remote designs that the text says cannot hold a cylinder. Fig. 2c's 3D surface is hard to read. The section is very dense. Most of my re-derivation required filling in steps.

Scores. The kinematic core is correct and elegant, and its numbers are internally consistent with the figures. The falsification plan is concrete. The main gap is between what is measured (path deviation, preload) and what is claimed (Jacobian). That gap can be fixed for Stage 2. As a Stage-1 submission I lean to accept.
