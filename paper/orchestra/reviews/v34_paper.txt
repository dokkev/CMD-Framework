         Same Motion, Different Contact: Design of a
               Contact-Centric Robot Hand
                                     Author Names Omitted for Anonymous Review


   Abstract—Research hands for dexterous manipulation are
built as chains of servos, one at each joint. Such hands leave
room for a contact interface only at the fingertip, and a finger
redesigned to carry a thicker interface changes with it, so the
effect of the interface itself cannot be measured. This paper
proposes contact-centric design, which moves the servo off the
joint and redesigns the finger for a new interface by preserving
the motion of its contact surfaces, dispensing with the skeleton
as the redesign target. An analysis of the pole of the joint shows
that a fixed axis above the pad’s mount cannot preserve this
motion, whereas a crossed four-bar can. Design studies show
that the resulting finger keeps more contact surface than a
servo-at-joint finger at every pad thickness and required flexion,
and that its redesigns stay at a fraction of the fixed-axis bound.
